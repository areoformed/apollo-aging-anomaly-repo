Abstract

Background

Older people are the fastest-growing age group, with the highest risk of cognitive impairment. This study assessed the prevalence and associated factors with cognitive impairment in community-dwelling older people.

Methods

Older people were interviewed and accomplished through sociodemographic and health questionnaires. The quantitative variables were described by mean and standard deviation or median and interquartile range. The significance level adopted was 5 % (p < 0.05). The association between the quantitative variables was evaluated using the Pearson or Spearman correlation coefficients.

Results

The research population comprised 165 long-lived adults aged ≥80. The youngest one was 80, and the oldest one was 94 years old. The participants were 84.8 ± 3.6 years old, female (63 %) with a mean of education of 2.9 ± 1.8 years. A poor performance in the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) was found in 58 (35.2 %) individuals when adjusted for educational level. After adjustment for confounding factors, body mass index (BMI) (p = 0.09), total older adults' income (up to 1 minimum wage [mw], p = 0.023; over 1 to 2 mw, p = 0.023), functional disability (Moderate dependence 75 %, p = 0.038; Moderate dependence 50 %, p = 0.081; Moderate dependence 25 %, p = 0.054), and the anxiety scale (p = 0.032), remained associated with cognitive impairment.

Conclusions

This study showed that BMI, total older adults' income, functional disability, and anxiety are related to cognitive impairment in long-lived adults. This study has some limitations, such as the fact that it is a cross-sectional study, the reduced number of individuals, and the fact that there were no comparisons among different ages and populations.

1. Introduction

According to the Brazilian Institute of Demographics, the elderly population is increasing. In 2022, life expectancy was estimated to reach 75.6 years for women and 70.8 years for men (IBGE – Instituto Brasileiro De Geografia E Estatística, 2020). Along with aging, there are many physiological changes, including changes in neurological function and predisposition to cognitive impairment (Mattson and Arumugan, 2018).
Although very common with aging, cognitive impairment is not universal. It is known that there are many risk factors could lead to this. The main risk factor is age itself. Long-lived populations are more susceptible to cognitive impairment, and the dementia prevalence increases considerably after 80 years old, reaching 44 % after 95 years old. Besides age itself, with aging come many other risk factors, such as hypertension, depression, and diabetes (de Freitas, 2016; Chen et al., 2022).
Hypertension, according to the American Heart Association, is defined as tensional systolic levels above 129 mmHg and/or tensional diastolic levels above 80 mmHg (Whelton et al., 2017; Iqbal and Jamal, 2023). It leads to vascular endothelial damage resulting in changes in brain circulation, generating neuronal damage. Besides that, it was shown that intensive blood pressure control leads to better cognitive performance (The SPRINT MIND Investigators for the SPRINT Research Group, 2019). Another common pathology that comes along with aging and hypertension, is the diabetes (American Diabetes Association Professional Practice and Older Adults, 2024).
A study showed that diabetes alone could increase the risk of Alzheimer's disease development, and high levels of blood glucose lead to cognitive impairment and worse cognitive performance (Fan et al., 2017). This happens because high blood glucose levels lead to vascular damage, which in turn damages noble organ, such as the brain (Fan et al., 2017). Along with diabetes and hypertension, obesity is a common aging disorder. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), obesity is a chronic complex disease defined by excessive fat deposits that can impair health. When the elderly has hypertension, diabetes, and obesity, he is known to have metabolic syndrome (Li et al., 2021; Safaei et al., 2021).
Metabolic syndrome is not a disease but a term that highlights traits that may increase the risk of disease. It is highly prevalent along with aging. Study shows that rats with metabolic syndrome genes are predisposed to dementia because of the inflammatory reactions that those pathologies cause, leading to all those damages mentioned in hypertension and diabetes (Li et al., 2021; Samson and Garber, 2014). Besides those metabolic factors, there are social and psychological factors, such as depressive disorder (Nousen et al., 2013; Zhou et al., 2024).
According to WHO, it is estimated that 4,4 % of the world population suffers from depressive disorder, being this the most common mood disorder (World Health Organization, 2021). Several studies relate major depressive disorder (MDD) with dementia risk. This probably occurs because of the similar physiological features of both diseases, such as neurodegeneration, inflammation, and production of neurotransmitters that could lead to neuronal damage (Tetsuka, 2021; Wei et al., 2019; Chow et al., 2022).
Along with MDD can come anxiety disorder, which is also a risk factor for cognitive impairment, as this disease affects the same brain areas that depression and dementia and has similar physiological processes. Besides that, in the early stages, all those diseases have similar features (Botto et al., 2022). When the subject has those diseases and cognitive impairment, this leads to a loss of independence and autonomy that can be analyzed by the functional scales, like basic activities of daily life (BADLs) and instrumental activities of daily life (IADLs) (de Freitas, 2016).
BADLs include the capacity of older people to eat by themselves, take a bath by themselves, and other factors that keep their independence. IADLs reflect the ability of the elderly to interact with the environment and have social independence. With cognitive decline, those abilities decrease, which is a risk factor for dementia (de Freitas, 2016).
So, as the population is getting older and having more pathologies, it is important to understand the relationship between aging, cognitive impairment, and comorbidities to help improve the quality of life of the elderly. Many questions remain unanswered like if aging alone would be a risk factor, or if in the long lived, the risk factors for the cognitive decline remain the same. To shed light on these questions, this study aimed to assess the prevalence and associated factors with cognitive impairment in community-dwelling elderly from 80 years old.

2. Methods

2.1. Study design

It was designed as a cross-sectional study that was developed with ≥80 years older adults and was based in two cities in Brazil where >10 % of the population is aged ≥60 years old. The study was conducted in two cities (Sideropolis and Treviso) in the south of Santa Catarina, Brazil. The cities have a high lifespan and a prevalent ethnicity of German and Italian. According to the last Census by the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics of 2010, Siderópolis has an estimated population of 13,499. The elderly population over 80 years old was 208 inhabitants in Sideropolis. Treviso has an estimated population of 3706 inhabitants. The elderly population over 80 years old was 70 inhabitants in Treviso. Both add up to a total of 278 elderly people over 80 years old. These two cities have the most long-lived elderly people and the least heterogeneity in the south of Santa Catarina. The elderly population over 80 years old is considered a fourth age group (da Silva, 2016), we studied this group to investigate the process of healthy and pathological aging since the prevalence of mild cognitive impairment increases significantly with age (9.5 % for 60–69 years; 14.6 % for 70–79 years; and 23.6 % for 80 years and older) (Lu et al., 2021).
Participants were recruited through the public health service. The selection of participants was of the census type, in which all individuals aged 80 years or over, who use the Primary Health Care service (Family Health Strategies) – SUS - who were registered in the Citizen's Electronic Record (PEC) from the target municipalities of the study, were recruited. The health service and the researchers identified and contacted subjects about participating in the study. At this time, the public health agents explained the study, according to a previous meeting and information from the researchers to the health agents. As soon as they agreed to participate, a home visit was scheduled by the researcher together with a public health agent. And then, the researchers again explained the study to older people and family members in the presence of the public health agent.

2.2. Ethical aspects

This study followed the ethical precepts contained in Resolution 466/2012 of the National Health Council, which deals with the Research Norms and Guidelines involving human beings (Brasil., 2012). This is part of a Macro-Project that was submitted to the Research Ethics Committee of the University (UNESC) and has approval number 1,032,742. The older adults who participated in the study were given detailed information about the research and signed the Term of Free and Informed Consent form before inclusion.

2.3. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

2.3.1. Inclusion

Individuals of both sexes; aged 80 years or over; residents in Sideropolis and Treviso and registered in the Primary Health Care service of those locations.

2.3.2. Exclusion

Participants were not registered in the Primary Health Care service, and had partial visual impairment (from 40 to 60 % impairment) or total visual loss; severe hearing loss (those that cannot hear sounds below 80 dB, on average, and deep, when not listening to sounds emitted with an intensity of <91 dB); a diagnosis of schizophrenia or other psychotic disorders; conversive disorder (with weakness, paralysis, abnormal movement, speech symptoms, convulsive seizures, sensory loss or mixed symptoms); neurocognitive disorders (delirium, Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease); drug-induced movement disorders and adverse drug effects (neuroleptic-induced parkinsonism, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, acute dystonia, acute akathisia, late dyskinesia, late dystonia, tardive akathisia, postural tremor); stroke with speech impairment (dysarthria paralytic, cerebellar dysarthria and extrapyramidal dysarthria, dyslalia, dyslexia, aphasia, suppression of language or stereotypy, verbal stereotypy); stroke with impaired movement of upper limbs (arms and hands); or any other condition that negatively influences normal performance in the evaluations used in this study. All exclusion criteria were confirmed from previous medical diagnoses informed by the participant or family member.
Therefore, all the older adult people who did not meet the exclusion criteria and agreed to participate were included in the study after signing the Informed Consent Form.

2.4. Procedures

The data were collected by trained researchers while visiting the household of older adults from August 2015 to September 2016. Before the interviews, the researchers explained the study, its objectives, and how the questionnaires would be performed. All participants' doubts were answered. There were no elderly with a lack of understanding of the study. However, because of their advanced age, most of them were accompanied by family members during the interviews, ensuring the ethical responsibility of the study. The interviews took an average of 1 h, and the research instruments were applied.

2.4.1. Instruments and questionnaires

Instruments and questionnaires to collect data are described as follows:
  • -
    The first questionnaire aimed to collect information about the subjects' sociodemographic and health conditions. For this purpose, the participants answered sociodemographic questions such as age, sex, race, area of residence (urban or rural area), civil status, educational level, type of family, monthly income, body mass index (BMI), the practice of physical activity. Then, self-rated health condition, presence of clinical comorbidities, use of medicines, sleep pattern, and memory loss.
  • -
    The functional disability or scale of activities of daily living was assessed using the Assessment of Basic Activities of Daily Living (BADLs) and Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADLs): the first was modified to evaluate health status and social performance, while the IALDs, assesses the functional capacity itself (Katz, 1970; Lawton and Brody, 1969). For BADLs the score can vary from 20 to 140 points, and IADLs can vary from 5 to 35 points. When the points of the two categories are added, a minimum score of 25 and a maximum of 175 points are obtained, which may characterize the dependence from completely dependent to complete independence.
  • -
    The Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE): is a well-known test of global cognitive functioning that provides a brief snapshot of aspects of orientation, repetition, attention, language, visuoconstruction, and short-term recall. Scores are influenced by age and the subject's level of education, and normative corrections have long been available. The MMSE score can vary from 0 to 30, the highest scores are related to better cognitive performance. However, cutoff points are considered: 13 for illiterates, 18 for 1 to 7 years of study, and 26 for ≥8 years of study (Bertolucci et al., 1994). In Brazil, validation and evaluation studies using this instrument have been widely utilized in research (Bertolucci et al., 1994; Folstein et al., 1975; Brucki et al., 2003).
  • -
    Depression Scale (CES-D): It was developed by the National Institute of Mental Health and has a satisfactory accuracy in measuring the screening of individuals with depressive mood in primary health services (Silveira and Jorge, 1998). Consisting of 20 items, classified on a frequency scale (0= rarely, 3= most or all the time), the score ranges from 0 to 60, and a cutoff point of 16 or above is used to identify individuals that are prone to developing depression (Radolff, 1977).
  • -
    Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD-7): This scale is used to evaluate, monitor, and diagnose anxiety. This test is composed of 7 items that are arranged on a scale of four points: 0= no time, 3= almost every day. The score varies from 0 to 21, and to characterize the presence of anxiety symptoms, the subject should have a value equal to or >10 (Spitzer et al., 2006).

2.5. Statistical analysis

The mean and standard deviation or interquartile range and median described the quantitative variables. Absolute and relative frequencies described the categorical variables. The measures of central tendency and dispersion were reported for continuous variables.
To compare means, the t-Student test for independent samples was applied. In cases of asymmetry, the Mann-Whitney test was used. Pearson's chi-square or Fisher's exact tests were used to compare proportions.
For control of confounding factors, the multivariate Poisson Regression model was applied for cognitive impairment. The measure of effect used was the Prevalence Ratio (PR) in conjunction with the 95 % confidence interval. The criterion for the entry of the variable in the multivariate models was that they had a value p ≤ 0.20 in the bivariate analysis, and for permanence in the final model, they had a value p ≤ 0.10.
The significance level adopted was 5 % (p < 0.05), and the data analysis were performed using the SPSS program (version 21.0).
The association between the quantitative variables was evaluated using the Pearson correlation coefficients (symmetric distribution) or Spearman (asymmetric distribution) tests.

3. Results

According to the last Census by the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics of 2010, Siderópolis and Treviso have an estimated population of 278 individuals over 80 years old. One hundred eighty-eight individuals used the SUS and were registered in the PEC. However, 17 did not meet the inclusion criteria, and six declined to participate in the study. So, the study had a sample of 165 long-lived people, with a loss of 12 %.
The sample was 165 subjects: 125 individuals from Siderópolis-SC and 40 from Treviso-SC. The data were analyzed together because both cities have similar profiles. Table 1 shows the sociodemographic characterization: the mean age was 84.8 ± 3.7 years, 63 % were women, 98.8 % declared having a white skin color, 65.5 % resided in a rural area, and about the civil status, 53.9 % were widowers. Most of the elderly lived with their relatives 45.5 %. Regarding levels of education, the older adults were found to have a low level of education, considering that the median was 3 years. In relation to total income, 56.1 % receive between 1 and 2 minimum wages. The BMI presented an average of 25.6 ± 4.6, and 38.8 % of the older adults practice some type of physical activity.

4. Discussion

The present study is aimed at long-lived elderly. Older people are the age group with the highest growth in population in Brazil and the world (Nogueira et al., 2010; North and Sinclair, 2012). This study predominantly comprises women, agreeing with the global demographic profile, showing the feminization of old age. In Brazil, 55.7 % of its 20 million older people are women (Nogueira et al., 2010; Aliberti et al., 2018; Singer et al., 2019; Chalermsri et al., 2023; Ijaz et al., 2023; de Sousa et al., 2023). The predominant ethnicity of the individuals is white, which reflects the profile of the community, due to their region being colonized by Europeans, especially Italians.
The population has low levels of education, three years on average, and the income is 1 or 2 minimum wage per person. This data corroborates with the Brazilian data of the last analysis of Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE – Instituto Brasileiro De Geografia E Estatística, 2020).
It was observed that the most prevalent clinical comorbidity was SAH, at 75.8 %, corroborating with data from another study (Ijaz et al., 2023; Lai et al., 2018; Monti et al., 2021). A paucity of studies have evaluated the prevalence of hypertension in this age group. Therefore, it becomes necessary medical treatments and social investments in this area.
When asked about medication use, it's possible to observe a more significant percentage of use, 95 % of those older people are dependent on medicine daily. There is to a trend of increasing polypharmacy and excessive polypharmacy among elderlies, especially at the age of 75+ and among women (Bartošovič et al., 2023; Nishino et al., 2023). Some studies, mostly clinical studies, have turned their attention to this population because most are polypharmaceutical patients and demand more attention from professionals to change their pharmacological treatment, either to increase, change or decrease medication (Bartošovič et al., 2023; Jackson, 2007; Hughes et al., 2016).
Along with modernity, a few complications have become more prevalent, such as sleep disturbances, and most commonly observed in aged people (Tian and Li, 2017; Hu et al., 2023). In our study, 41.2% complained about their sleep. 65 % of this population takes residence in rural areas. A study by Dong et al. (Dong et al., 2018), analyzed the residents of rural areas in China and observed that when comparing the quality of sleep of people who live in rural areas and urban areas, the people living in rural areas have poorer quality of sleep. This data is not sufficiently clear, but one hypothesis is that the aging people who live in rural areas continue to work after retirement. The job could be stressful to these residents and prejudicial to their sleep.
Beyond that, when comparing our sociodemographic data with the memory test, the number of people living with dementia increases continuously, and globally, the number of people with dementia is expected to be 65.7 million by 2030 (World Health Organization, 2021; Jing et al., 2016). Cognitive impairment was found in 35.2 % of the participants. In a study by Juncos-Rabadan and contributors (Juncos-Rabadán et al., 2014), those who were older than 80 years presented a cognitive impairment score from the MMSE of 54.5 %. These results are expected since age and few years of education are risk factors for cognitive impairment (LaRoche et al., 2014; Ryan et al., 2019). Once again, the study represents this population well, corroborating with those studies in other countries.
The work of Beydoun and contributors (Beydoun et al., 2014), evaluating 247 studies, found that in addition to low levels of schooling, an inferior economic position was also associated with lower cognitive function. The same findings were seen in the present study, suggesting that older adults with incomes lower than three minimum wages showed a worsening of cognitive functions.
Another factor that may be associated with cognitive impairment in older adults is the area of residence, whereby older adults living in rural areas have a higher prevalence of dementia (6.24 %) than those living in urban areas (4.38 %), which agreed with the findings of the present study. A study performed by Masika (Masika et al., 2023) showed that older people in rural areas in Tanzania have poor cognitive functions and are at high risk of further cognitive decline. This could result from less formal education for the elderly and less cognitive stimuli since they usually need to start working early in life.
Functional capacity is also directly related to cognitive abilities (Trindade et al., 2013; Andrade et al., 2017; Jang et al., 2022). The present study observed that older adults with a moderate dependence of 75 % have twice the prevalence of cognitive impairments. These findings corroborate with other studies, such as one by Lee and contributors (Lee et al., 2008). Since it was a cross-sectional study, it is hard to evaluate if those participants were less functional because of the cognitive impairment or had cognitive impairment because they were less functional, being a limitation of the study.
Another interesting finding to be highlighted in this study is related to generalized anxiety in long-lived adults. Those who presented anxious symptoms had a 64 % higher prevalence of cognitive impairment. In addition, the prevalence of GAD in older adults with cognitive impairment was 20.7 %. Depression and anxiety are known risk factors for cognitive impairment (Safaei et al., 2021; Samson and Garber, 2014), which could justify those findings. Besides that, in the early stages of cognitive impairment, the symptoms are similar to depression and anxiety, which could be confounding. Since those diseases interfere with the ability necessary to complete cognitive performance tasks, the results of those participants could lead to a wrong interpretation. More studies are required to confirm those hypotheses, but these findings support the relation between those pathologies.
This study has some limitations. First, this is a cross-sectional study. This analysis does not enable a cause–effect relationship to be inferred. Second, the number of individuals aged 80 or older recruited in this study was limited. Third, there were no comparisons among different ages and populations. Fourth, the dependence between factors is assessed by the effect of multicollinearity, which only exists when the correlation or association between the factors is very strong, being stronger than the association with the outcome. We assessed multicollinearity through some associations present in the literature, which were not strong enough to influence the final model. For example, there is a correlation between obesity and depression (Milaneschi et al., 2019), but this is not observed in our study. However, the methodology employed followed all of the epidemiological criteria.

5. Conclusion

As far as we know, this study is one of the few existing studies on this long-lived population in Brazil. It suggests that there can be a relationship between higher BMI and better cognitive performance, but since it was a cross-sectional study, it cannot confirm this relation. Besides that, low income, and functional disability were related to worst cognitive performance.
Another finding was the relationship between anxiety symptoms and cognitive impairment. It is suggested that those pathologies have a relation. Still, there are not many studies to confirm that, so this study comes to corroborate this association. However, since it was a cross-sectional study, It cannot define causality, which is one of the limitations. Besides that, it evaluated a particular population, and maybe the data cannot be extrapolated to all people.
Even so, it is a relevant study since it provides essential information on the population characteristics, especially regarding cognitive performance and cognitive impairment risk. This information is necessary to provide medical, social, and political decisions that can improve the quality of living and the social development of this region and other similar regions in the country.

We aimed to determine the global prevalence of cognitive impairment in older medical inpatients and explore variations by assessment method and region.
    We searched MEDLINE (Ovid), Embase, Cochrane Central, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, and PsycINFO for relevant articles. We included patients ≥ 60 years old admitted to hospitals and studies examining prevalence of cognitive impairment. Excluded were elective surgical patients and non-hospital settings. Cognition was assessed by validated cognitive assessment tools, diagnostic instruments, or prior diagnosis. The primary outcome was the pooled prevalence of cognitive impairment in older patients admitted to hospitals. Subgroup analyses were conducted based on cognitive assessment tools, geographic regions, study type, and sample size.
    Fifty-six studies (n = 62,853) were included. Mean age was 79.8 ± 7.7 years with 56% female. Meta-analysis revealed substantial variability across studies, with pooled prevalence estimates demonstrating high heterogeneity. The prevalence of newly suspected cognitive impairment/dementia using the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders and/or validated screening tools was 38% (95% CI: 33%–42%). The prevalence of pre-diagnosed cognitive impairment/dementia documented from medical records was 24% (95% CI: 19%–29%). The prevalence of dementia was higher in Western countries than Eastern countries [41% (95% CI: 32%–50%) vs 27% (95% CI: 21%–33%)].
    Cognitive impairment is prevalent among older medical inpatients. The variability in prevalence was dependent on assessment method and geographic region. Given the substantial heterogeneity and low to very low certainty of the evidence available, these prevalence estimates should be interpreted with caution.

  • Automated brain segmentation and volumetry in dementia diagnostics: a narrative review with emphasis on FreeSurfer

    2024, Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience
    Dementia can be caused by numerous different diseases that present variable clinical courses and reveal multiple patterns of brain atrophy, making its accurate early diagnosis by conventional examinative means challenging. Although highly accurate and powerful, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) currently plays only a supportive role in dementia diagnosis, largely due to the enormous volume and diversity of data it generates. AI-based software solutions/algorithms that can perform automated segmentation and volumetry analyses of MRI data are being increasingly used to address this issue. Numerous commercial and non-commercial software solutions for automated brain segmentation and volumetry exist, with FreeSurfer being the most frequently used.
    This Review is an account of the current situation regarding the application of automated brain segmentation and volumetry to dementia diagnosis.
    We performed a PubMed search for “FreeSurfer AND Dementia” and obtained 493 results. Based on these search results, we conducted an in-depth source analysis to identify additional publications, software tools, and methods. Studies were analyzed for design, patient collective, and for statistical evaluation (mathematical methods, correlations).
    In the studies identified, the main diseases and cohorts represented were Alzheimer’s disease (n = 276), mild cognitive impairment (n = 157), frontotemporal dementia (n = 34), Parkinson’s disease (n = 29), dementia with Lewy bodies (n = 20), and healthy controls (n = 356). The findings and methods of a selection of the studies identified were summarized and discussed.
    Our evaluation showed that, while a large number of studies and software solutions are available, many diseases are underrepresented in terms of their incidence. There is therefore plenty of scope for targeted research.
