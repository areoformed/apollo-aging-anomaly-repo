# NASA Group 2 ("The New Nine") Selection Criteria: Direct Quotes

## Overview

NASA's second astronaut group was selected in 1962. Unlike the Mercury Seven selection, NASA did not publish a formal equivalent of Release 59-113 documenting the selection tests. What follows are direct quotes from memoirs, oral histories, NASA histories, and secondary sources about the selection process, medical tests, and criteria used.

---

## 1. Neil Armstrong Oral History (NASA JSC)

**Source:** Neil A. Armstrong, NASA Johnson Space Center Oral History Project, interviewed by Dr. Stephen E. Ambrose and Dr. Douglas Brinkley, September 19, 2001.
**URL:** https://historycollection.jsc.nasa.gov/JSCHistoryPortal/history/oral_histories/ArmstrongNA/ArmstrongNA_9-19-01.htm
**Also at:** https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/62281main_armstrong_oralhistory.pdf

> "Well, I don't think the community of flight medicine and flight physiology knew very much what they needed to do at that point."

> "There were widespread predictions that humans could not survive in space, for a variety of reasons, both physical, physiological and mental and psychological, all kinds of reasons. So they didn't really know exactly what to test for, I think, so they did everything."

> "They did every test known to man. [Laughter] Not necessarily fun. Survivable."

**Context note (from Hansen biography, cited in Wikipedia):** "At Brooks Air Force Base at the end of June, Armstrong underwent a medical exam that many of the applicants described as painful and at times seemingly pointless."

**Citation for context note:** James R. Hansen, *First Man: The Life of Neil A. Armstrong* (New York: Simon & Schuster, 2005). Referenced in Neil Armstrong Wikipedia article.

## 2. Jim Lovell Oral History (NASA JSC)

**Source:** James A. Lovell Jr., NASA Johnson Space Center Oral History Project, interviewed May 25, 1999.
**URL:** https://historycollection.jsc.nasa.gov/JSCHistoryPortal/history/oral_histories/LovellJA/LovellJA_5-25-99.htm
**Also at:** https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/lovellja-5-25-99.pdf

> "At Brooks Air Force Base [Texas]... Air Force physical. Much more practical... looking at what's wrong and what's right. And I had no problems passing it."

**Context:** Lovell had been rejected from the Mercury Seven selection due to a temporarily elevated bilirubin count. He described the Group 2 medical exam at Brooks as a straightforward "Air Force physical" -- a marked contrast to the Lovelace Clinic tests he had previously undergone for Mercury selection.

From *Lost Moon* (later retitled *Apollo 13*), regarding his earlier Mercury rejection:

> "I have five men out there who don't have a bilirubin problem, and 26 more on the way who probably don't."

**Citation:** Jim Lovell and Jeffrey Kluger, *Lost Moon: The Perilous Voyage of Apollo 13* (Boston: Houghton Mifflin, 1994). Referenced in Jim Lovell Wikipedia article.

## 3. Pete Conrad -- Selection Contrast with Mercury

**Source for Mercury rejection details:** Nancy Conrad and Howard A. Klausner, *Rocketman: Astronaut Pete Conrad's Incredible Ride to the Moon and Beyond* (New York: New American Library/Penguin, 2005). Details referenced in Pete Conrad Wikipedia article and multiple secondary sources.

On Mercury-era testing at the Lovelace Clinic (1959):

> During a Rorschach inkblot test, Conrad told the psychiatrist that one blot card "revealed a sexual encounter complete with lurid detail." When shown a blank card, "he turned it around, pushed it back and replied, 'It's upside down.'"

The flight surgeons' verdict on Conrad:

> "not suitable for long-duration flight"

**Variant phrasing from other sources:** "not fit for long duration space flight"

On the 1962 Group 2 selection:

> "the physical exam was done in one day and there were no inkblots or stress tests much to Pete Conrad's pleasure"

**Citation:** This quote appears across multiple secondary sources citing *Rocketman* and the Pete Conrad Wikipedia article. The exact sourcing to a page number in *Rocketman* requires the physical book.

**Additional context from secondary sources:**

> "Conrad found the medical tests less invasive, and in June 1962 he was selected to join NASA."

**Source:** Pete Conrad Wikipedia article, citing *Rocketman*.

## 4. Deke Slayton -- *Deke!* Autobiography

**Source:** Donald K. "Deke" Slayton with Michael Cassutt, *Deke! U.S. Manned Space: From Mercury to the Shuttle* (New York: Forge/Tom Doherty Associates, 1994).

On changing the age requirement:

> "One thing that got tougher was that we dropped the maximum age from 40 to 35. In Mercury, we were looking at a programme that would conclude in three years. We knew that Apollo would be going until 1970 at the very least."

On the overall quality of the group:

> "That second group was probably the best all-around group ever put together."

**Additional Slayton detail from secondary sources:** Slayton insisted on receiving letters of recommendation from each candidate's last employer. This was a new requirement not present in Mercury selection.

**Citation for letters requirement:** Referenced in the AmericaSpace article "The Best All-Around Group" (2012) and the NASA Astronaut Group 2 Wikipedia article, both citing *Deke!*

## 5. NASA Official History -- 60th Anniversary Article

**Source:** John Uri, "60 Years Ago: NASA Selects A Second Group of Astronauts," NASA History, September 17, 2022.
**URL:** https://www.nasa.gov/history/60-years-ago-nasa-selects-a-second-group-of-astronauts/

> "On April 18, 1962, NASA launched a widely-advertised campaign for its Flight Crew Training Program to select five to 10 new astronauts."

> "From the 253 applications received by the June 1 deadline (one arrived a week late, but managers chose to accept it anyway), NASA whittled the field down to 32 finalists who underwent intensive medical examinations at Brooks Air Force Base in San Antonio."

> "During this phase, physicians disqualified one candidate for exceeding the height requirement."

> "In July and August, the 31 finalists appeared before the selection board for further tests and interviews by Coordinator of Astronaut Activities Donald K. 'Deke' Slayton and the other members of the board."

## 6. Wikipedia -- NASA Astronaut Group 2

**Source:** "NASA Astronaut Group 2," Wikipedia (compiled from multiple cited sources including *Deke!*, *First Man*, *Rocketman*, *Forever Young*, and NASA histories).
**URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_2

On the selection panel:

> "A three-person selection panel consisting of Mercury Seven astronauts Alan Shepard and Deke Slayton, and NASA test pilot Warren J. North"

On medical eliminations:

> "another four were eliminated on the basis of ear, nose and throat examinations"

On the test location and nature:

> "The finalists were sent to Brooks Air Force Base in San Antonio for medical examinations. The tests there were much the same as those employed to select the Mercury Seven."

On the "charm school":

> "The Air Force ran the candidates through a brief training course in May 1962 on how to speak and conduct themselves during the NASA selection process. The candidates called it a 'charm school.'"

## 8. Ed White -- Selection Test Details

**Source:** Ed White (astronaut) Wikipedia article and Grokipedia article (compiled from multiple sources).
**URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_White_(astronaut)

> "White excelled in the physical evaluations, passing centrifuge simulations that exposed candidates to up to 10 g-forces to mimic launch and reentry stresses, as well as zero-gravity parabolic flights aboard a modified C-131 aircraft to test tolerance for microgravity conditions."

**Caution:** This detail appears in the Grokipedia article on Ed White. It may describe pre-selection training experience rather than the Group 2 selection tests specifically. Cross-reference needed against primary sources. ## 9. David Scott -- Group 3 Quote (Comparative)

**Source:** David Scott, referenced in NASA Astronaut Group 3 Wikipedia article.

> Candidates were relieved "not to be subjected to the indignities endured" by their predecessors in having "every bodily orifice probed and checked."

**Context:** This refers to Group 3 (1963), not Group 2, but is valuable for understanding the trajectory. Group 3 tests included "cardiograms, running on treadmills, electrocephalograms, and blood sugar tests." This implies Group 2 was an intermediate step between Mercury's invasive testing and Group 3's more streamlined approach.

## 10. Selection Criteria -- Formal Requirements

**Source:** Multiple (NASA official history, Wikipedia, Spaceline.org, Drew Ex Machina).

The April 18, 1962 announcement specified:

> "United States citizen, 6 feet (1.8 m) or less in height, with a degree in engineering or the physical or biological sciences, and recommended by their employer."

Specific changes from Mercury Seven criteria:

> "The height restriction was raised to 6 feet tall; the required hours of flight time was lowered to 1,000 [from 1,500]; the education requirement mandated a degree in physical science, biological science or engineering; the maximum age was lowered to 35 [from 40]; and, perhaps most importantly, civilian pilots -- including women -- were allowed to apply."

**Citation:** Drew Ex Machina, "NASA Selects the 'New Nine' -- September 17, 1962," September 17, 2020. URL: https://www.drewexmachina.com/2020/09/17/history-brief-nasa-selects-the-new-nine-september-17-1962/

## 11. Psychological Testing Evolution -- NASA Technical Report

**Source:** "Forty Years of Psychological and Psychiatric Selection of NASA Astronauts," NASA Technical Reports Server (NTRS), 2000.
**URL:** https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20000085964

> "Between 1959 and 1987, the psychiatric evaluation of astronaut candidates evolved from a 30-hour intensive examination evaluating applicants for psychopathology and studying their performance under stress, to a 2-hour clinical interview whose structure and contents were determined by the individual examiner."

**Context:** This establishes the trend line. Mercury selection used ~30 hours of psychiatric evaluation. By the Shuttle era it was ~2 hours. Group 2 (1962) fell at the beginning of this reduction, as corroborated by Conrad's testimony about the elimination of inkblots and stress tests.

## 12. Psychology of Space Exploration -- NASA SP-2011-4411

**Source:** Douglas A. Vakoch, ed., *Psychology of Space Exploration: Contemporary Research in Historical Perspective*, NASA SP-2011-4411.
**URL:** https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/607107main_psychologyspaceexploration-ebook.pdf

> "By the end of 1962, a pivotal decision was made: NASA officially prohibited research teams from collecting data on astronaut job performance."

> "From the Gemini program through the early Space Shuttle era, the primary psychological input was a brief interview with psychiatrists whose goal was simply to 'select-out' any signs of overt psychopathology. The nuanced, performance-oriented 'select-in' model of Mercury was abandoned."

**Context:** This establishes that the Group 2 selection in 1962 coincided with a fundamental shift in NASA's approach to psychological evaluation -- from "select-in" (identifying positive traits under stress) to "select-out" (screening for disqualifying pathology).

## 14. Selection Process Timeline (Composite)

Assembled from NASA official history and Wikipedia:

- **April 18, 1962:** NASA publicly announces it is accepting applications for 5-10 new astronauts. Unlike Mercury (conducted in secret), this is widely advertised to aircraft companies, government agencies, and the Society of Experimental Test Pilots.
- **June 1, 1962:** Application deadline. 253 applications received. (Armstrong's arrived a week late; Walter C. Williams had Richard Day add it to the pile.)
- **May 1962:** Air Force candidates attend "charm school" -- briefing on NASA interview expectations.
- **Late June through mid-July 1962:** Medical examinations at Brooks Air Force Base, San Antonio, TX. One candidate disqualified for height. Four eliminated on ENT (ear, nose, throat) exams.
- **July-August 1962:** 31 remaining finalists appear before the selection board (Slayton, Shepard, North) at the Manned Spacecraft Center in Houston for interviews and further evaluation.
- **September 14, 1962:** Slayton personally calls each selectee by phone.
- **September 17, 1962:** Public announcement at press conference, Cullen Auditorium, University of Houston (some sources say Ellington AFB).

## 15. What Changed from Mercury Selection

| Aspect | Mercury (1959) | Group 2 (1962) |
|--------|---------------|----------------|
| **Announcement** | Secret; military records screened | Public advertisement |
| **Eligibility** | Active-duty military test pilots only | Military and civilian test pilots |
| **Max age** | 40 | 35 |
| **Max height** | 5'11" | 6'0" |
| **Min flight hours** | 1,500 | 1,000 |
| **Education** | Engineering degree preferred | Degree in engineering, physical science, or biological science |
| **Medical facility** | Lovelace Clinic (Albuquerque, NM) | Brooks AFB (San Antonio, TX) |
| **Medical duration** | Multiple days; ~7.5 days per candidate | Approximately one day (per Conrad) |
| **Psychiatric testing** | ~30 hours; Rorschach, MMPI, stress tests, isolation, extensive interviews | Dramatically reduced; no inkblots, no stress tests (per Conrad) |
| **Selection panel** | NASA + military medical + psychiatric | Slayton, Shepard, North (pilot-dominated) |
| **Employer letters** | Not required | Required (Slayton's addition) |
