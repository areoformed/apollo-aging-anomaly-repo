# Research Log: NASA Group 2 ("The New Nine") Selection Criteria

**Date:** 2026-05-22
**Researcher:** Claude (automated web search)
**Objective:** Document exact medical tests, physical evaluations, psychological assessments, and stress tests used in the 1962 Group 2 astronaut selection.

---

## Key Finding

This is the hardest batch to document, as predicted. NASA did not publish an equivalent of Release 59-113 (the Mercury selection announcement document) for Group 2. The selection was publicly announced on April 18, 1962, but the original announcement text has not been located online. Most detail comes from astronaut memoirs and oral histories, not from NASA institutional documentation.

## What We Know About Specific Tests

### Confirmed for Group 2 selection (1962):
- **Medical examinations at Brooks AFB** -- multi-source confirmation
- **Physical exam** -- described as "Air Force physical" by Lovell, done in "one day" per Conrad sources
- **ENT (ear, nose, throat) exams** -- four candidates eliminated on this basis (Wikipedia, multiple sources)
- **Height measurement** -- one candidate disqualified for exceeding 6-foot limit
- **Interviews with selection panel** (Slayton, Shepard, North) at MSC Houston
- **No Rorschach inkblot tests** -- confirmed by Conrad contrast with Mercury (multiple sources)
- **No stress tests** (in Mercury sense: isolation, heat, cold, etc.) -- confirmed by Conrad (multiple sources)

### Possibly included but sourcing uncertain:
- **Centrifuge simulations (up to 10 g)** -- mentioned in Ed White Grokipedia article but may describe prior experience, not the selection test itself
- **Zero-gravity parabolic flights (C-131)** -- same caveat as centrifuge
- **Cardiograms, treadmills, EEGs, blood sugar** -- confirmed for Group 3 (1963) by David Scott quote; may also apply to Group 2

### Confirmed NOT included (changed from Mercury):
- **Rorschach inkblot tests** -- explicitly excluded (Conrad)
- **MMPI or equivalent personality batteries** -- implied by "no inkblots or stress tests"
- **Extended psychiatric evaluation** (~30 hours in Mercury) -- reduced to brief screening
- **Isolation chamber tests** -- part of Mercury stress tests, not mentioned for Group 2
- **Heat/cold endurance tests** -- part of Mercury, not mentioned for Group 2
- **Feet-in-ice-water test** -- Mercury-era, not mentioned for Group 2
- **Balloon-blowing exhaustion test** -- Mercury-era, not mentioned for Group 2
- **Lovelace Clinic involvement** -- testing moved to Brooks AFB

## Methodological Notes

- **WebFetch was denied** during this session, which meant I could not load and read full web pages. All quotes come from web search result snippets, which sometimes truncate or paraphrase. Quotes marked with full confidence have been cross-referenced across multiple search results.
- **The "painful and at times seemingly pointless" description** attributed to Hansen's *First Man* appears in the Wikipedia article on Neil Armstrong but is a paraphrase of multiple applicants' views, not a direct Armstrong quote. Armstrong's own words from the oral history ("every test known to man... not necessarily fun. Survivable.") are more characteristic of his understated style.
- **The "one day" physical exam claim** from Conrad sources may reflect Conrad's subjective comparison with Mercury rather than the literal duration of the Brooks examination period. The NASA 60th anniversary article places medical exams during "July and August," suggesting the full cohort was processed over weeks even if individual exams were shorter.
- **Ed White centrifuge/parabolic flight detail** requires verification. The Grokipedia source is AI-generated and may conflate White's prior experience (testing zero-G with Glenn/Slayton as passengers) with the selection tests themselves.
