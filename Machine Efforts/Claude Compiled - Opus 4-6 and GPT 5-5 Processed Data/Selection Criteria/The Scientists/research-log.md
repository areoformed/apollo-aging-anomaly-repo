## KEY FINDINGS

### What we established with direct quotes:

1. **Formal requirements for Group 4:**
   - US citizenship
   - Doctorate (PhD or MD) in natural sciences, medicine, or engineering
   - Born on or after August 1, 1930 (approx. age 35 max)
   - Height 6'0" (183 cm) or less
   - Must pass Class I Military Flight Status Physical
   - 20/20 uncorrected vision
   - No pilot experience required

2. **The NAS screening process:**
   - NAS defined scientific qualifications; MSC specified physical/psychological
   - 1,351 applications received
   - ~200 rejected for basic criteria (age, citizenship, height, vision)
   - ~400 forwarded to NAS (4 were women)
   - NAS board (Shoemaker-chaired) reduced to 16 names
   - 15 sent to Brooks AFB for medical exams (May 2, 1965)
   - NASA interview panel (May 12, 1965): Berry, Clark, Faget, North, Shepard, Slayton
   - 6 selected, announced June 27/28/29, 1965 (sources vary by 1-2 days)

3. **What changed from Mercury:**
   - Test pilot school: REQUIRED (Mercury) -> NOT REQUIRED (Group 4)
   - Flight hours: 1,500 hrs jet (Mercury) -> NONE (Group 4)
   - Education: Bachelor's (Mercury) -> Doctorate (Group 4)
   - Max height: 5'11" (Mercury) -> 6'0" (Group 4)
   - Max age: Under 40 (Mercury) -> Under ~35 (Group 4)
   - Physical stress testing: Extensive (Mercury: centrifuge, heat, cold, pressure, vibration, isolation, tilt table) -> Brooks AFB medical exam (Group 4: specific tests unknown from available sources)
   - Psychological evaluation: Continuous with psychologist cohabitation (Mercury) -> "Much less information" available (Group 4)

4. **What was NOT relaxed:**
   - Vision: Still 20/20 uncorrected (same as Mercury through Group 3)
   - Class I Flight Physical: Still required
   - Height: Relaxed from Mercury (5'11") to Groups 3-4 (6'0"), but this was a spacecraft engineering constraint, not health selection

### For the longevity analysis specifically:

The key interpretive question is whether the Class I Flight Physical alone (without Mercury-era stress testing) constitutes a meaningful health selection filter. If so, it partially explains Group 4 longevity despite "relaxed" selection. The Class I physical still screened for:
- Cardiovascular health
- Perfect uncorrected vision
- Neurological function
- Hearing
- General excellent health

This is a strong medical filter even without the stress testing gauntlet. The "relaxation" was primarily in dropping:
- Extreme physical endurance testing (centrifuge, heat, cold, pressure)
- Prolonged psychological observation
- Test pilot performance records (which served as de facto fitness/resilience screening)

The pilot experience itself was a form of health selection — test pilots had already survived years of high-G maneuvers, demonstrating cardiovascular resilience. Scientists had no such pre-screening. This makes Group 4's longevity outcomes more interesting, not less.
