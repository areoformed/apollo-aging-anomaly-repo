## 1. NASA SP-4214: "Where No Man Has Gone Before" (W. David Compton, 1989)

### Chapter 5-7: "Selection of the First Scientist-Astronauts"

**Division of responsibility between NAS and MSC:**
> "MSC officials and representatives of the National Academy of Sciences met in February to draft a plan for recruitment and selection. Agreement was reached that the Academy would define the scientific qualifications desirable in the candidates while MSC would specify the physical and psychological requirements."
>
> — NASA SP-4214, Ch. 5-7, https://www.hq.nasa.gov/pao/History/SP-4214/ch5-7.html

**Primary qualification:**
> "The primary requirement was a doctorate in medicine, engineering, or one of the natural sciences."
>
> — NASA SP-4214, Ch. 5-7

**No pilot requirement:**
> "No applicant had to be a qualified pilot; those accepted by the Space Science Board and by NASA would be assigned to the Air Force for a year of flying training."
>
> — NASA SP-4214, Ch. 5-7

**NAS screening and disappointment:**
> "An ad hoc committee of the Space Science Board (chaired in Hess's absence by Eugene Shoemaker) rigorously scrutinized about 400 of those who passed NASA's preliminary screening, finally sending only 16 names to NASA for final evaluation."
>
> — NASA SP-4214, Ch. 5-7

> "Shoemaker later recalled that the committee had been disappointed in the overall quality of the applications that came in. Not many of the scientists who applied came up to the rather high standards they set."
>
> — NASA SP-4214, Ch. 5-7

**Smaller group than planned:**
> "NASA was able to pick only 6 scientist-astronauts instead of 10 or more, as it had initially planned."
>
> — NASA SP-4214, Ch. 5-7

**Critical quote — Slayton's information deficit:**
> "MSC had hoped for a larger group to choose from; Slayton's selection board had much less information on the applicants' physical condition and psychological makeup than they had for military applicants, and the choices were consequently harder to make."
>
> — NASA SP-4214, Ch. 5-7

**Announcement:**
> "On June 27, 1965, NASA announced the names of its first scientist-astronaut candidates: two physicians, Duane M. Graveline and Lt. Cmdr. Joseph P. Kerwin, MC, USN, and four Ph.D. scientists, F. Curtis Michel, Edward G. Gibson, Owen K. Garriott, and Harrison H. Schmitt (who was generally known by his nickname, 'Jack')."
>
> — NASA SP-4214, Ch. 5-7

### Chapter 5-2: "Test-Pilot Astronauts" (for comparison)

**Group 2 changes (1962):**
> "Since the Gemini spacecraft would be bigger than Mercury, Deke Slayton devised his own set of selection criteria for the next group of astronauts, increasing the height limit and changing the age restriction. The maximum age was dropped from 40 to 35."
>
> — NASA SP-4214, Ch. 5-2, https://www.hq.nasa.gov/pao/History/SP-4214/ch5-2.html

## 2. Wikipedia: NASA Astronaut Group 4

**Application requirements (compiled from multiple cited sources):**
> "Applicants had to be United States citizens with a doctoral degree in the natural sciences, medicine, or engineering, or equivalent professional experience demonstrating advanced scientific proficiency."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_4

**Age criterion:**
> "Birth date on or after August 1, 1930."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

**Height requirement:**
> "Height of 6 feet 0 inches (1.83 m) or less."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

> "The height restriction was firm, an artifact of the size of the Apollo spacecraft."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

**Vision and medical standard:**
> "Candidates had to be able to pass a Class I Military Flight Status Physical. This required 20/20 uncorrected vision."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

> "The helmets astronauts wore could not accommodate glasses, and contact lenses were considered to be unsuitable in space."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

**Application materials:**
> "Candidates had to have copies of their academic transcripts from each university they had attended, along with Educational Testing Service scores and medical history sent directly to the Astronaut Selection Board of the NAS by December 31, 1964, along with medical examination results."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

**Initial screening:**
> "A total of 1,351 applications were received by the deadline. About 200 of these were rejected for failing to meet the basic age, citizenship, height or vision criteria. The names of 400 applicants (four of whom were women) were forwarded to NAS to review their academic qualifications."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

**NAS board composition:**
> "The NAS selection board consisted of Allan H. Brown, Loren D. Carlson, Frederick L. Ferris, Thomas Gold, H. Keffer, Clifford Morgan, Eugene Shoemaker, Robert Speed and Aaron Waters."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

**Medical examination at Brooks AFB:**
> "On May 2, 1965, they were sent to the United States Air Force School of Aerospace Medicine at Brooks Air Force Base, near San Antonio, Texas, for medical examinations."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

**NASA interview panel:**
> "The final step, on May 12, 1965, was an interview by the NASA selection panel, which consisted of Charles A. Berry, John F. Clark, Maxime Faget, Warren J. North and Mercury Seven astronauts Alan B. Shepard and Donald K. Slayton."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

**Historical significance:**
> "They were the first astronauts chosen on the basis of research and academic experience."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

**Pilot status of selectees:**
> "The only qualified pilots in the group were Michel, a former Air Force pilot, and Kerwin, a naval aviator. The other four were sent to Williams Air Force Base in Arizona to begin 55 weeks of flying training."
>
> — Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 4"

## 6. NASA History: Project Mercury Selection (1959) — FOR COMPARISON

### Requirements

> "Less than 40 years of age; less than 5 feet 11 inches tall; excellent physical condition; bachelor's degree or equivalent; graduate of test pilot school; and 1,500 hours of jet flight time."
>
> — NASA, "65 Years Ago: NASA Selects America's First Astronauts," https://www.nasa.gov/history/65-years-ago-nasa-selects-americas-first-astronauts/

> "Astronaut candidates had to be male, not older than 40 years of age, and not more than 5-foot, 11-inches in height and in excellent physical condition."
>
> — NASA, "Project Mercury Overview — Astronaut Selection," https://www.nasa.gov/history/project-mercury-overview-astronaut-selection/

### Lovelace Clinic Medical Tests (Phase 1)

> "Over 30 different laboratory tests collected chemical, encephalographic, and cardiographic data. X-ray examinations thoroughly mapped each man's body. The ophthalmology section and the otolaryngology sections likewise learned almost everything about each candidate's eyes, and his ears, nose, and throat."
>
> — NASA, "65 Years Ago: NASA Selects America's First Astronauts"

> "Special physiological examinations included bicycle ergometer tests, a total-body radiation count, total-body water determination, and the specific gravity of the whole body."
>
> — NASA, "65 Years Ago: NASA Selects America's First Astronauts"

### Wright-Patterson AFB Stress Tests (Phase 2)

> "At the Aero Medical Laboratory at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Dayton, Ohio, from February 15 through March 28, candidates underwent testing that included acceleration tests, vibration tests, heat tests, and loud noise tests, and were required to be placed in centrifuges and altitude chambers to assess their responses."
>
> — NASA, "Project Mercury Overview — Astronaut Selection"

> "Each candidate had to prove his physical endurance on treadmills, tilt tables, with his feet in ice water, and by blowing up balloons."
>
> — NASA, "Project Mercury Overview — Astronaut Selection"

> "The candidates were forced to spend an hour in a pressure chamber that simulated an altitude of 65,000 feet, and two hours in a chamber that was heated to 130 degrees Fahrenheit."
>
> — NASA, "Project Mercury Overview — Astronaut Selection"

> "Continuous psychiatric interviews, the necessity of living with two psychologists throughout the week, and extensive self-examination through a battery of 13 psychological tests for personality and motivation, and another dozen different tests on intellectual functions were also part of the selection process."
>
> — NASA, "Project Mercury Overview — Astronaut Selection"

> "The purpose of the testing program at Wright Field was to determine the physical and psychological capability of the individual to respond effectively and appropriately to the various types of stresses associated with space missions."
>
> — NASA, "Project Mercury Overview — Astronaut Selection"

## KEY ANALYTICAL NOTES FOR LONGEVITY STUDY

1. **The Class I Flight Physical was retained.** Despite dropping the test pilot requirement and all flight experience requirements, Group 4 candidates still had to pass a Class I Military Flight Status Physical with 20/20 uncorrected vision. This is a meaningful medical filter.

2. **The grueling Mercury-era stress tests were NOT part of the Group 4 process.** Mercury candidates underwent centrifuge, heat chamber (130 deg F for 2 hours), altitude chamber (65,000 ft for 1 hour), ice water immersion, tilt tables, vibration tests, isolation chambers, and continuous psychiatric evaluation. Group 4 candidates went to Brooks AFB for medical examinations — the sources describe this as medical examinations, not the stress-testing gauntlet of Mercury.

3. **Slayton had "much less information" on physical/psychological fitness.** This is the critical quote for the longevity analysis. The selection board for Group 4 was working with substantially less data about each candidate's physical and psychological resilience than they had for military astronaut selections.

4. **The NAS screening was entirely academic.** The NAS board (Shoemaker et al.) evaluated scientific qualifications only. Physical/psychological assessment was NASA's responsibility, but the available information was limited compared to military applicants whose service records contained extensive medical and performance data.

5. **Height requirement unchanged from Group 3.** The 6-foot maximum was an engineering constraint (Apollo spacecraft dimensions), not a health selection criterion. This was the same for Groups 3 and 4.

6. **Age limit approximately the same.** "Born after August 1, 1930" = approximately under 35, similar to Groups 2 and 3. This was NOT relaxed for scientists.

7. **Despite all this, Group 4 members show high longevity.** This is the puzzle: reduced physical selection stringency, yet comparable or superior longevity outcomes.
