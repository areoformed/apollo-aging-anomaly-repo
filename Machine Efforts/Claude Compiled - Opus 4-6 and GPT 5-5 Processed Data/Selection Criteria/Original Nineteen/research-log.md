## Key Findings

### What Is Well-Documented

1. **Selection funnel numbers:** 5,000 applications -> 351 qualifying -> 159 further consideration -> 44 medical exams -> 19 qualified -> 19 selected.
2. **Formal requirements:** Citizenship, born on/after Dec 1 1929, under 6'0", BS in STEM, test pilot school OR 1000 hrs jet time, Class I flight physical, uncorrected 20/20 vision.
3. **Medical exam location and dates:** Brooks Air Force Base, San Antonio, TX. January 7 - February 15, 1966.
4. **Some specific tests:** Rorschach, encephalograms, treadmills, centrifuge, hot water immersion (hand), cold water in ears (caloric vestibular test).
5. **Scoring system:** 30-point scale (10 academics, 10 pilot performance, 10 character/motivation), 18 of 30 from interview.
6. **Selection panel:** Slayton (chair), Shepard, Young, Collins, C.C. Williams, Warren North, Max Faget.
7. **Key decision:** Slayton took all 19 qualified candidates due to attrition concerns after See/Bassett deaths.
8. **Age requirement change:** Raised from 34 (Group 3) to 36 (Group 5). Otherwise criteria "much the same" as Group 3.

### What Is NOT Well-Documented (Gaps)

1. **Complete test battery at Brooks for Group 5.** Wikipedia lists only Rorschach, encephalograms, treadmills, centrifuge, hot water, cold water. Group 3's documented tests (cardiograms, blood sugar, hypoxia, darkened room rotation) almost certainly also applied but are not explicitly confirmed for Group 5.
2. **Whether Group 5 tests were less invasive than Mercury.** Group 3's Wikipedia article explicitly states "less invasive than those conducted for previous astronaut selections." No equivalent statement exists for Group 5. The shift from Lovelace Clinic (Mercury) to Brooks AFB (Groups 2-5) is documented, but the degree of test reduction between Group 3 and Group 5 is not.
3. **Detailed psychological assessment battery.** Only Rorschach is named for Group 5. Mercury used 12+ distinct psychological instruments (Rorschach, TAT, draw-a-person, sentence completion, Wechsler, Miller analogies, Raven matrices, etc.). Whether Group 5 used a reduced battery is unknown from available sources.
4. **Stress tests.** Mercury used extensive stress tests (Harvard step, tilt table, isolation chamber, centrifuge, heat chamber, partial pressure suit, cold pressor, complex behavior simulator). Only the centrifuge and a hot-water/cold-water test are documented for Group 5. The rest are unconfirmed.
5. **Firsthand astronaut accounts of the 1966 medical process.** No memoir-level quotes about the Group 5 Brooks AFB experience were accessible via web search. The books (Duke, Worden, Mitchell, Collins) are flagged for manual review.

## Methodological Notes

1. **Wikipedia is the richest single source for Group 5 selection details.** The NASA Astronaut Group 5 article appears to draw heavily from Collins' *Carrying the Fire* and possibly Burgess' Springer Praxis books, though specific footnotes were not extractable via web search.

2. **The "Inquisition" quote attributed to Don Lind appears in Wikipedia's Group 5 article** but its original source (Lind biography, oral history, or interview) was not locatable online. It is widely reproduced but not independently verifiable without the primary source.

3. **Group 5 medical testing is less documented than any earlier group.** Mercury has the NASA Fact Sheet. Group 2 has the "much the same" comparison. Group 3 has a specific test list. Group 5 has a partial test list and the "Inquisition" anecdote, but no comprehensive catalog.

4. **The shift from Lovelace Clinic to Brooks AFB** happened between Mercury (1959) and Group 2 (1962). By Group 5 (1966), Brooks was the established venue. The nature of the tests evolved: Mercury's stress testing at WADC (Wright-Patterson) appears to have been absorbed into the Brooks program by Group 3, eliminating the two-site evaluation.

5. **Selection scoring was consistent from Group 3 onward.** Slayton's 30-point system (academics/pilot performance/character) was used for Groups 3, 4, and 5. The system allocated 60% of points to interview performance (18 of 30), making subjective panel assessment the dominant factor.
