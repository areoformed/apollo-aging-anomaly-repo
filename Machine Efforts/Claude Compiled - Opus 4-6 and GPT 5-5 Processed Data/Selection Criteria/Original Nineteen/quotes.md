## 1. Wikipedia: NASA Astronaut Group 5

**Source:** "NASA Astronaut Group 5," Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_5

### Formal Requirements

> Candidates had to meet these criteria:
> - Be a United States citizen
> - Born on or after December 1, 1929
> - Height of 6 feet 0 inches (1.83 m) or less
> - Bachelor's degree in physical/biological sciences or engineering
> - Graduate of armed forces test pilot school OR 1,000+ hours jet flying experience
> - Pass Class I flight physical with uncorrected 20-20 vision

### Application Funnel

> "About 5,000 applications were received by the deadline, and of these, only 351 met the key criteria."

> "From this group, 159 applicants, 100 of whom were military and 59 were civilians, were selected for further consideration."

> "Six women had applied, but none apparently met the key criteria, most likely because women were not allowed to fly military jet aircraft in the United States at this time."

> "Lieutenant Frank K. Ellis, a U.S. Navy aviator who had lost both legs in an air crash in July 1962, submitted an application, arguing that being a double amputee would not be a handicap in space. NASA was impressed with his tenacity, but he too was passed over."

### Medical Examinations at Brooks Air Force Base

> "44 candidates were selected to undergo medical examinations at Brooks Air Force Base at San Antonio, Texas, conducted between January 7 and February 15, 1966."

> "Psychological tests included Rorschach tests; physical tests included encephalograms, and sessions on treadmills and a centrifuge."

> "Other tests included some that [Don] Lind thought had been originated by the Inquisition, such as plunging a hand into hot water and having cold water poured into the ears."

### Selection Panel and Scoring System

> "The seven-member panel was chaired by Deke Slayton and included astronauts Alan Shepard, John Young, Michael Collins, and C.C. Williams, plus NASA test pilot Warren North and spacecraft designer Max Faget."

> "Slayton employed a points system for assessing the candidates, the same that he had used for previous selections. Each candidate was given a score out of 30."

> "Ten points were for 'academics,' which were broken down into one point for IQ, four for academic degrees and qualifications, three for NASA aptitude tests, and two for the results of a technical interview."

> "Ten points were for 'pilot performance,' which were broken down into three points for flying record, a point for a test pilot rating, and six points for a technical interview."

> "The remaining ten points were for 'character and motivation.'"

> "The interview took about an hour for each of the candidates, and eighteen of the thirty points were awarded for the interview."

### Final Selection Decision

> "When the scores were tallied, Fred Haise came out with the highest score."

> "In all, 19 candidates were rated as qualified. Young and Collins were shocked when Slayton said that he would take all 19."

> "A reason for the larger-than-expected cohort was that the astronaut corps' attrition rate was double the 10% NASA had expected, including the deaths in February of Elliot M. See and Charles Bassett in the 1966 NASA T-38 crash."

### Group Composition

> "Of the nineteen, four were civilians: Brand, Haise, Lind and Swigert. Seven were from the USAF: Majors Givens, Irwin and Pogue, and Captains Duke, Engle, Roosa and Worden."

### Nickname Origin

> "John Young labeled the group the 'Original Nineteen' in parody of the original Mercury Seven astronauts."

### Age Requirement Change

> "The criteria were much the same as those for NASA Astronaut Group 3 in 1963, except that the age requirement was raised from 34 to 36."

## 2. Wikipedia: NASA Astronaut Group 2 (Comparison)

**Source:** "NASA Astronaut Group 2," Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_2

### Medical Testing Comparison to Mercury

> "The finalists were sent to Brooks Air Force Base in San Antonio for medical examinations, and the tests there were much the same as those employed to select the Mercury Seven."

> "One candidate was rejected for being '2 inches (5 cm) too tall.' Four others were 'eliminated on the basis of ear, nose and throat examinations.'"

### Selection Criteria

> "Five minimum requirements governed eligibility:
> 1. '1,500 hours test pilot flying time' with military test pilot school graduation or equivalent NASA/industry experience
> 2. High-performance jet aircraft proficiency
> 3. College degree in engineering or physical/biological sciences
> 4. U.S. citizenship, under 35 years old, maximum 6 feet height
> 5. Employer recommendation"

### Gus Grissom on Selection Philosophy (Group 2)

> "We are picking experienced test pilots...because they have experience in dealing with new machines, unusual situations" and possessed "the engineering background that we're looking for."

## 3. Wikipedia: NASA Astronaut Group 3 (Comparison)

**Source:** "NASA Astronaut Group 3," Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_3

### Medical Examinations -- Progressive Relaxation

> "The physical examinations were less invasive than those conducted for previous astronaut selections."

### Specific Tests at Brooks (Group 3, 1963)

> "Tests included cardiograms, running on treadmills, electroencephalograms, and blood sugar tests."

> "Hypoxia tests were carried out to see how they reacted to a loss of oxygen."

> "They were spun about in darkened rooms to test their resistance to motion sickness."

> "Ice water was poured into one ear to test how the inner ears reacted to such an imbalance."

### Scoring System Origin

> "Slayton employed a points system for assessing the candidates. Ten points each were allocated to academics, pilot performance, and character and motivation, for a possible score of thirty points."

### Key Criteria Change (Group 3)

> "The test-pilot qualification, while still preferred, was no longer a prerequisite, with 1,000 hours of jet-aircraft pilot time accepted as a substitute."

### Selection Anecdote

> "Slayton initially selected 13 names. When presented to MSC director Robert R. Gilruth, aerospace engineer Maxime Faget objected to the unlucky number, so 'Slayton added the next candidate, Walter Cunningham, to the list,' resulting in 14 astronauts."

## 4. NASA Release No. 59-113: Mercury Astronaut Selection Fact Sheet (April 9, 1959)

**Source:** NASA Release No. 59-113, "Mercury Astronaut Selection Fact Sheet," April 9, 1959 (PDF in project archive)

*Note: This is the baseline against which all later group selections are compared.*

### Lovelace Clinic Medical Examinations (7.5 days per candidate)

> "General physical requirements were established by the NASA Life Sciences Committee; since all those examined are active test pilots it was not anticipated that any would be disqualified as physically unfit. Rather, degrees of physical soundness were obtained and evaluation was dependent upon a comparison of each man to his fellow candidates."

> "To establish a comparative yardstick, the Lovelace program began with a complete aviation and medical history and extended to these areas: Hematology and pathology (blood and study of tissues); Roentgenology (X-ray consultations); Ophthamology [sic] (eyes); Otolaryngology (ears, nose and throat); Cardiology (heart and circulation); Neurology and Myology (nerves and muscles); General internal medicine; Related laboratory studies."

### Dynamic Tests (First Use in Astronaut Selection)

> "To assess the candidate's abilities under load, Lovelace physicians developed a series of dynamic tests which were used for the first time in the Project Mercury selection process."

> "Capacity under load, or body efficiency, was determined from a correlation of the subject's physical competence with his pulmonary function, total body radiation count, specific gravity of the body, blood volume, water volume, lean body mass and detection of tiny congenital openings between the chambers of the heart."

### Wright Air Development Center (WADC) Psychological/Stress Testing

> "The next step in the selection process was a minute determination of the candidate's psychological makeup and an estimate of his ability to cope with stresses expected in space flight."

> "The Air Force, with the assistance of Army and Navy specialists, conducted psychological and stress measurements at the Wright Air Development Center Aeromedical Laboratories, Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Dayton, Ohio."

WADC evaluation areas:
> "Personality evaluation; Behavioral sciences; Stress and fatigue; Accelerative forces; Equilibrium and vibration; High energy noise; Low barometric pressure; Thermal stress; Anthropology."

### Mercury Final Selection Philosophy

> "As in the case of the Lovelace examinations, results were not a matter of passing or failing, but instead were measures of how one candidate compared with all others."

> "The seven ultimately selected were chosen as a result of physical, psychological and stress tolerence [sic] abilities and because of the particular scientific disciple [sic], or specialty, each represents."

### Lovelace Appendix: Detailed Clinical Examinations

> "-Laboratory tests: hemoglobin (measure of oxygen carrying red pigment); hematocrit (examination of blood by use of a centrifuge); grouping; Rh factor; serology (examination of blood serums); sedimentation rate (analysis of urine deposits); stool examinations; urinalysis; gastric analysis; cholesterol (substance present in gallstones, heart ailments, etc.); liver function test; urinary steroid excretion (measure of the hormones, acids and poisons); blood nitrogen; blood protein; protein-bound iodine; special serum studies; throat culture, and chemical examination of body outputs, and blood counts."

> "-X-rays: chest, large intestine, sinuses, spine, stomach, esophagus, teeth and heart. Moving pictures were taken of the heart to determine any artery calcification."

> "-Eyes: history, dilation, visual fields, tonometry (measure of inner pressure on the eyes), slit lamp, dynamic visual acuity, depth perception, night vision, and photography of conjunctival vessel (eye membrane) and retina."

> "-Ears, nose and throat: examination of throat and nasal passages; audiogram with and without background noises; speech discrimination and voice tape recording."

> "-Heart: cardiograms of heart muscle contraction, heart stroke volume and heart sounds; measure of the chest which overlies the heart."

> "-Nerves and muscles: general neurologic examination with muscle testing; electric stimulation of the nerves to determine response; measure of any nerve abnormality; tracing of electric currents produced by the brain."

## 8. Space Safety Magazine: "Historical Evolution of Astronaut Selection"

**Source:** Space Safety Magazine, https://www.spacesafetymagazine.com/space-exploration/astronaut-cosmonaut/historical-evolution-astronaut-selection/

> "Psychological and stress evaluations were conducted by the Air Force at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, including tests where candidates were locked alone in a totally dark room for an extended time."

> "NASA determined that astronaut selection should be based on both scientific and operational criteria, with flight safety considerations taking precedence when conflicts arose."

> "Psychiatric evaluation techniques changed from independent reviews with no standardization to use of standard psychological tests identifying psychiatric disorders and traits that could impact safety."

> "Disqualifying conditions are continuously revised to meet medical advancements; for example, visual acuity requirements have been progressively relaxed with advances in contact lenses and corrective eye surgery."

## Summary: Group 5 Selection Tests (Documented)

### Medical Tests at Brooks Air Force Base (Jan 7 - Feb 15, 1966)

**Physical:**
- Encephalograms (EEG -- brain electrical activity)
- Treadmill sessions (cardiovascular stress)
- Centrifuge (g-force tolerance)
- Cold pressor / caloric test ("plunging a hand into hot water and having cold water poured into the ears")

**Psychological:**
- Rorschach inkblot tests

**Not explicitly documented for Group 5 but confirmed for Group 3 at same facility (Brooks):**
- Cardiograms
- Blood sugar tests
- Hypoxia tests (oxygen deprivation response)
- Rotation in darkened rooms (motion sickness / vestibular)
- Ice water in ear (caloric vestibular test)

### Selection Scoring (30-point system, same as Groups 3-4)

| Category | Points | Breakdown |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| Academics | 10 | 1 IQ + 4 degrees + 3 aptitude tests + 2 technical interview |
| Pilot Performance | 10 | 3 flying record + 1 test pilot rating + 6 technical interview |
| Character/Motivation | 10 | Interview-based |

18 of 30 points came from the interview (approximately 1 hour per candidate).

### Selection Funnel

| Stage | Count |
|-------|-------|
| Applications received | ~5,000 |
| Met key criteria | 351 |
| Selected for further consideration | 159 (100 military, 59 civilian) |
| Medical examinations at Brooks | 44 |
| Rated qualified | 19 |
| Selected | 19 (all qualified) |
