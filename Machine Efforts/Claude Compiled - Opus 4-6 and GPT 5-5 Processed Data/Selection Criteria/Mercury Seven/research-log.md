# Research Log: NASA Group 1 (Mercury Seven) Selection Criteria

**Date:** 2026-05-22
**Researcher:** Claude (automated web search)
**Objective:** Document exact medical tests, physical evaluations, psychological assessments, and stress tests used in the 1959 Mercury astronaut selection. Direct quotes only, with full citations. Look BEYOND NASA Release 59-113.

---

## Key Finding

The Mercury Seven selection is indeed the best-documented of all astronaut batches, but the richest sources are books (memoirs and Burgess's Springer monograph) whose full text could not be extracted via web search. The NASA official online sources (SP-4201, SP-4003, the Project Mercury Overview page) provide the structural framework and test categories. The Vintage Space blog provided the most granular detail on specific cardiovascular test protocols (Flack test, Valsalva overshoot, Cold Pressor parameters). The OSU Glenn collection and secondary sources (AmericaSpace, History.com) provided the most vivid Lovelace Clinic procedure descriptions. The Hersch/Springer chapter and Ruff obituary provided the psychiatric evaluation details.

**Major gap:** The full text of NASA SP-4003 Chapters 5-6 is available as a PDF online but could not be read during this session (WebFetch was denied). This is the single most important unread source.

2. **NASA.gov -- "65 Years Ago: NASA Selects America's First Astronauts" (2024)** -- John Uri article. Provides 65,000-foot altitude chamber detail, 130F heat chamber, Feb 7 start date, "one candidate failed a blood test" detail, Lovell eliminated.

## What We Know vs. What We Don't

### Confirmed with specifics:
- **Lovelace laboratory tests:** "over 30" identified by category but NOT individually enumerated in any accessible source. Blood, urine, stool, EKG, EEG, X-rays, eye exam (4 hrs), ENT, barium enema, stomach tube, bicycle ergometer, tilt table, total body water, specific gravity, radiation count, body volume (submersion), liver dye, prostate exam, "Steel Eel" lower bowel exam, nerve reflex via electrical pulse.
- **Wright-Patterson cardiovascular tests:** All 7 named and described (Flack, Valsalva, Partial Pressure Suit, Tilt Table, Harvard Step, Cold Pressor, Treadmill).
- **Wright-Patterson environmental stress:** Pressure chamber (65,000 ft / 1 hr), heat chamber (130F / 2 hrs), noise (145 dB + math), isolation (dark/silent + EEG leads), centrifuge, vibration.
- **Wright-Patterson psychological:** Rorschach, TAT, MMPI, "Who Am I?" TST. 13 total personality/motivation tests (4 named), 12 intellectual/aptitude tests (not individually named beyond 3 Voas tests from Phase II).
- **Washington aptitude tests:** Miller Analogies (IQ), Minnesota Engineering Analogies, Doppelt Mathematical Reasoning.

### Known to exist but not individually named:
- 9 of the 13 personality/motivation tests (only Rorschach, TAT, MMPI, TST named)
- 12 intelligence/aptitude tests at Wright-Patterson (count confirmed, individual names not captured)
- "Five different schedules" of Lovelace medical evaluation (structure referenced but schedule contents not enumerated)
- Specific anthropometric measurements taken at Wright-Patterson
- Exact centrifuge g-force levels during Mercury selection
- Exact isolation chamber duration

### Not found / uncertain:
- **Sperm count or fertility testing** -- not confirmed in any source
- **"Draw a Person" test** -- not confirmed for Mercury (common in era but not specifically documented)
- **Sentence completion test** -- not confirmed for Mercury
- **"Complex behavior simulator"** -- device name not found in Mercury context
- **"Multi-disorientation device"** -- device name not found in Mercury context
- **Exact isolation chamber duration** -- "hours" per History.com and PopSci but no specific number

## Methodological Notes

- **WebFetch was denied** during this session, which meant full web pages could not be loaded and read. All quotes come from web search result snippets. Some snippets may truncate or slightly paraphrase the original text. Quotes from NASA official pages and secondary sources were cross-referenced across multiple search results for consistency.
- **The "Steel Eel" nickname** appears in the OSU Friendship 7 collection materials attributed to the Glenn memoir/archival sources. Not confirmed in any other source. May be specific to Glenn's account or may be a term of art among the Lovelace staff.
- **The Jim Lovell inventory** ("livers injected with dye, inner ears filled with cold water...") is widely attributed to Lovell but the exact original source (book page, oral history timestamp) was not pinpointed. It appears across many secondary sources suggesting it may originate from *Lost Moon* or an interview.
- **"Over 30 laboratory tests" vs. specific count**: NASA's official page says "over 30 different laboratory tests" for Lovelace. No source found gives an exact count or a complete numbered list. The SP-4003 PDF is the most likely source for this.
- **The Vintage Space blog** (Amy Shira Teitel) proved to be the single most useful secondary source for specific test protocols and parameters. Her posts cite NASA technical documents and are generally well-sourced, though page-level citations to the original documents were not always captured in search results.
- **The distinction between Lovelace tests and Wright-Patterson tests** is important. Some secondary sources conflate the two. Lovelace = medical/diagnostic (blood work, imaging, body composition, ENT, ophthalmology). Wright-Patterson = stress/environmental (cardiovascular challenges, heat/cold/noise/isolation, psychological battery, aptitude testing, anthropometric measurements).
- **Pete Conrad's Rorschach rebellion** is one of the most widely cited anecdotes from the Mercury selection. It comes from *Rocketman* (Nancy Conrad, 2005). Conrad was rejected from Mercury, largely (per *Rocketman*) for his attitude during psychiatric evaluation. He was later selected for Group 2 in 1962 when psychiatric testing was drastically reduced.
