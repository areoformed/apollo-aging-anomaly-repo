## 1. APPLICATION ANNOUNCEMENT AND REQUIREMENTS

### Call for Applications (September 26, 1966)

> "NASA issued a call for applications for scientist astronauts on September 26, 1966, with the National Academy of Sciences conducting initial screening of candidates and forwarding their applications to NASA for selection."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"; corroborated by NASA.gov "55 Years Ago" article by John Uri

> "The deadline for applications was midnight, January 8, 1967."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

### Formal Requirements

> "Candidates had to: Be a United States citizen, or become one by March 15, 1967."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

> "Born on or after August 1, 1930."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

> "Height no more than 6 feet (1.83 m) -- this requirement was firm due to Apollo spacecraft size constraints."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

> "Hold a doctorate in natural sciences, medicine, or engineering, or equivalent."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

> "Meet physical requirements for pilots."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

> "The announcement said that exceptions might be made for outstanding candidates, but height requirement was firm, an artifact of the size of the Apollo spacecraft."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"; also Grokipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

> "Maximum age for the new crop of Scientist Astronauts was raised to not older than 40."
-- Spaceline.org, "NASA Group 6 Scientist Astronauts"; corroborated by PLOS One analysis (PMC5531584)

> "Qualifications included a doctorate in natural sciences, medicine, or engineering; being under 35 years old; under 183 cm height; and excellent health."
-- Wireworld, "Brian O'Leary, Ex-Astronaut"
[NOTE: Wireworld states 35, other sources state born on/after Aug 1 1930 (i.e., under 37 at selection). Age limit was relaxed/abolished for this group per Spaceline. Discrepancy across sources.]

> "In all other respects, qualifications during this draft were identical to those for NASA Group 4."
-- Spaceline.org, "NASA Group 6 Scientist Astronauts"

### Application Documentation

> "Applicants had to provide supporting documentation in the form of academic transcripts, multiple references, a research bibliography and medical history."
-- Grokipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"; also Wikipedia

## 2. NAS-NRC SCREENING PROCESS

> "From the 923 applications received by the January 8, 1967 deadline, the Academy narrowed the field down to 69 candidates based on their academic credentials."
-- NASA.gov, John Uri, "55 Years Ago: NASA Selects 11 Scientist-Astronauts" (2022); also Wikipedia, Grokipedia

> "The NAS selection panel went through the applications and forwarded 69 candidates on to NASA for consideration."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

> "In 1967, the National Academy of Sciences screened candidates, and NASA selected 11 finalists, all of whom had Ph.D. or M.D. degrees, all of whom were civilians, and were required to attend jet pilot school for a year before beginning their regular training program."
-- National Academies, "Preparing for the High Frontier" (2011), Chapter 1

### Historical Context: NAS Screening for First Scientist-Astronaut Group (Group 4, 1965)

> "An ad hoc committee of the Space Science Board (chaired in Hess's absence by Eugene Shoemaker) rigorously scrutinized about 400 of those who passed NASA's preliminary screening, finally sending only 16 names to NASA for final evaluation."
-- NASA SP-4214, "Where No Man Has Gone Before," Ch. 5-7 (on Group 4; NAS process was similar for Group 6)

> "The Board's Ad Hoc Committee on Scientific Qualifications of Scientist-Astronauts began work in May, 1964, to establish scientific criteria for applicants."
-- NASA SP-4214, "Where No Man Has Gone Before," Ch. 5-7

## 3. MEDICAL AND PSYCHOLOGICAL TESTING AT BROOKS AFB

> "This group underwent medical examinations at the School of Aerospace Medicine at Brooks Air Force Base in San Antonio in the spring of 1967, further narrowing the number of candidates to 21."
-- NASA.gov, John Uri, "55 Years Ago: NASA Selects 11 Scientist-Astronauts" (2022)

> "Candidates were subjected to a week of physical and psychological tests and examinations at the USAF School of Aerospace Medicine at Brooks Air Force Base in San Antonio, Texas."
-- Grokipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"; also Wikipedia; also Space Safety Magazine

> "Background and security checks were conducted by the United States Civil Service Commission."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"; also Grokipedia

> "In 1967, when the Moon race was still on, two bright, energetic scientists who were competing for selection into the astronaut corps found themselves roommates at Brooks Air Force Base in Texas, where detailed medical tests were done."
-- National Academies, "Preparing for the High Frontier" (2011), Chapter 1

### Specific Tests (from Don Lind's account of Group 5 selection at Brooks, 1966 -- same facility and era)

> "Psychological tests included Rorschach tests; physical tests included encephalograms, and sessions on treadmills and a centrifuge. Other tests included some that Lind thought had been originated by the Inquisition, such as plunging a hand into hot water and having cold water poured into the ears."
-- Don Lind, NASA JSC Oral History, May 27, 2005 (describing Group 5 Brooks testing, 1966; same facility used for Group 6 in 1967)

### Brian O'Leary on Psychological Testing

> "Brian O'Leary describes the entire psychological testing component as a relatively smart person who is trying to figure out what the psychologist wants to hear to maximize his chances of getting through the process."
-- Wireworld, "Brian O'Leary, Ex-Astronaut" (paraphrasing O'Leary's "The Making of an Ex-Astronaut," 1970)

### Kirkus Review on O'Leary's Account of Testing

> "In between was a series of physical and psychological tests of torture chamber thoroughness, recaptured by O'Leary with a fine comic touch."
-- Kirkus Reviews, review of "The Making of an Ex-Astronaut" by Brian O'Leary (1970)

### Brian O'Leary on the Centrifuge

> "Brian emerged from NASA's centrifuge, where Brian experienced 9 Gs."
-- Wade Frazier, "Brian O'Leary's Biography" (ahealedplanet.net), describing O'Leary's training

## 4. INTERVIEW PHASE AT MSC (HOUSTON)

> "That group came to the Manned Spacecraft Center (MSC), now NASA's Johnson Space Center, in Houston, for interviews with the selection committee. They also received a demonstration flight in the back seat of a T-38 jet aircraft so doctors could assess their reaction."
-- NASA.gov, John Uri, "55 Years Ago: NASA Selects 11 Scientist-Astronauts" (2022)

### Interview Committee Composition

> "Interviews were conducted at the Manned Spacecraft Center near Houston, Texas, by Mercury Seven astronaut Deke Slayton, NASA's Director of Flight Crew Operations; the Chief of the Astronaut Office, Alan Shepard; Wilmot N. Hess, the NASA Director of Science and Applications; NASA physician Charles Berry; scientist astronaut Owen Garriott; Maxime Faget, the chief engineer of the Apollo Program; and Robert F. Thompson, the head of the Apollo Applications Program."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"; corroborated by NASA.gov and Grokipedia

### T-38 Demonstration Flight

> "Finally, they were taken to Ellington Air Force Base where they were given a test flight in a Northrop T-38 Talon to measure their degree of comfort with flying."
-- Grokipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"; also Wikipedia

## 5. SELECTION ANNOUNCEMENT (August 4, 1967)

> "On August 4, 1967, NASA and the National Academy of Sciences announced the selection of 11 new scientist-astronauts. Chosen from 923 applicants, the Group 6 astronauts included three medical doctors, three astronomers, two physicists, one geophysicist, one electrical engineer, and one chemist."
-- NASA.gov, John Uri, "55 Years Ago: NASA Selects 11 Scientist-Astronauts" (2022)
[NOTE: Some sources give August 11 as announcement date. The discrepancy appears across Wikipedia (Aug 11) and NASA.gov/John Uri (Aug 4).]

> "Director of Flight Crew Operations Deke Slayton had planned to hire 20 to 30 new scientist-astronauts, but NASA found that only 11 of the 923 applicants were qualified, and hired them all."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

### Age Waivers

> "Karl Henize applied for the first scientist-astronaut group, but was denied because the age limit was 35 and he was 37. However, in 1967, NASA abolished the age limit, and Henize was selected as a scientist-astronaut by NASA in August."
-- Wikipedia, "Karl Gordon Henize" (paraphrased from multiple Wikipedia sources)

> "Two members -- William E. Thornton and Karl Henize -- exceeded the age limit but received waivers for exceptional qualifications, approved by Robert R. Gilruth and Chris Kraft."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

### Naturalized Citizens

> "The group included the first two naturalized U.S. citizens selected as astronauts, Chapman from Australia and Llewellyn from Wales."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

## 6. ARRIVAL AT MSC AND RECEPTION

> "The group reported for duty at MSC on September 18, 1967, increasing the number of active astronauts to 56."
-- NASA.gov, John Uri, "55 Years Ago: NASA Selects 11 Scientist-Astronauts" (2022)

> "When the group reported to the Manned Spacecraft Center, Slayton told them that 'We don't need you around here.'"
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

> "He offered to accept their resignations, and promised ground assignments if they did not quit, but warned them to not fool themselves that they would soon fly in space."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

> "During their first meeting with Director of Flight Operations Donald K. Slayton, he frankly informed them that it may be some time before they could expect a flight assignment. The many pilot-astronauts and the scientist-astronauts from the 1965 selection already in the program had priority for assignments to a diminishing number of flight opportunities."
-- NSS, "In Excess: The Travails of 1967's Astronaut Class"

> "In the year since NASA released the announcement, the agency's budget cuts had reduced the number of flights, especially Apollo Applications Program opportunities."
-- NSS, "In Excess: The Travails of 1967's Astronaut Class"

> "With self-deprecation, the men named themselves the 'XS-11', or 'Excess Eleven'."
-- Wikipedia, "NASA Astronaut Group 6"

## 7. BRIAN O'LEARY: SELECTION EXPECTATIONS AND RESIGNATION

### Initial Contact

> "O'Leary's saga started when he passed a Berkeley bulletin board with a leaflet announcing 'Opportunities for Scientists as Astronauts' and began in earnest with a July 1967 telephone call from Captain Alan Shepard."
-- NSS, "Infamous Spaceflight Controversies: 'I Guess Flying Just Isn't My Cup Of Tea'"

### O'Leary's Expectations About Selection Requirements

> "I was aware of the test-pilot emphasis in the program, but I felt optimistic about my potential situation for several reasons: the increase in the number of scientist-astronauts, the feeling of inevitability of science predominating the post-Apollo program with flights coming up in about three years, the greater relative role of the National Academy of Sciences, the relaxation of physical requirements, and the removal of the jet-flying requirement."
-- Brian O'Leary, "The Making of an Ex-Astronaut" (1970), as quoted in NSS and Wireworld articles

### Jet Training Misrepresentation

> "The program was sold to the scientists who applied as a way that they could still spend 25% of their time doing the sciences, as well as not requiring them to go through flight training, but after they had already started, it was explained that jet training would be mandatory."
-- NSS, "Infamous Spaceflight Controversies" (paraphrasing O'Leary)

### O'Leary's Resignation Call

> "I've spent a month now at flight school, have flown fifteen hours and soloed, and after much soul-searching I have decided to resign from the program. I guess flying just isn't my cup of tea."
-- Brian O'Leary to Deke Slayton, April 22, 1968, as quoted in NSS, "Infamous Spaceflight Controversies"

> "O'Leary remembered the conversation as friendly, and within hours he dictated his official resignation statement to NASA Public Affairs Officer Paul Haney."
-- NSS, "Infamous Spaceflight Controversies"

### Deke Slayton on O'Leary's Selection

> "In his autobiography 'Deke!', Deke Slayton claims that Brian O'Leary slipped through the gaps of psychological testing."
-- Wireworld, "Brian O'Leary, Ex-Astronaut" (citing Slayton & Cassutt, "Deke!: U.S. Manned Space from Mercury to the Shuttle," 1994)

## 8. STORY MUSGRAVE: UNIQUE BACKGROUND

> "In 1967, as an expert on cardiovascular and exercise physiology, Musgrave was picked by NASA as one of a group of scientist-astronauts to serve on future space missions."
-- Britannica, "Story Musgrave"

> "In August 1967, Musgrave was selected by NASA to be among the first cohort of astronaut-scientists. Until then, astronauts had been chosen from the ranks of military test pilots."
-- Academy of Achievement, "Story Musgrave, M.D."

> "Musgrave joined the Marine Corps in 1953 and served as an aviation electrician and instrument technician and as an aircraft crew chief. While serving in the Marines, he completed his GED."
-- Wikipedia, "Story Musgrave"

[NOTE: Musgrave's background was anomalous within Group 6. He had USMC enlisted service (not officer/pilot) plus an MD from Columbia, an MS in physiology, and additional degrees in mathematics, chemistry, and business. He was the only Group 6 member with any military background, though selected as a scientist, not a pilot.]

## 9. POST-SELECTION TRAINING REQUIREMENTS

> "After initial orientation and academic instruction, in March 1968 the group began 13 months of jet-pilot training."
-- NASA.gov, John Uri, "55 Years Ago" (2022); also Wikipedia

> "Training commenced with six weeks in the piston engine Cessna T-41 Mescalero, then moved on to twenty in the subsonic jet Cessna T-37 Tweet, and finally to twenty-seven weeks in the Northrop T-38 Talon."
-- Spaceline.org, "NASA Group 6 Scientist Astronauts"

> "During which two elected to resign."
-- NSS, "In Excess: The Travails of 1967's Astronaut Class" (O'Leary and Llewellyn)

### Llewellyn's Departure

> "Llewellyn needed to learn to fly jets, and was not able to fly the jet with the cockpit blacked out."
-- Wikipedia, "Anthony Llewellyn" (paraphrased)

## 10. NASA POLICY ON SCIENCE VS. OPERATIONS

> "Because of the complex and difficult operational requirements and crew safety, whenever conflict exists between operational and scientific requirements, flight safety considerations demand that the scientific requirements be subordinate to the operational requirements."
-- Space Safety Magazine, "Historical Evolution of Astronaut Selection" (quoting NASA policy)

## 11. GROUP COMPOSITION (for reference)

Full list of the eleven selected:
1. Joseph P. Allen IV -- physicist (Ph.D., Yale)
2. Philip K. Chapman -- physicist/astronautical engineer (Sc.D., MIT) -- Australian-born, naturalized
3. Anthony W. England -- geophysicist (Ph.D., MIT)
4. Karl G. Henize -- astronomer (Ph.D., Michigan) -- exceeded age limit, received waiver
5. Donald L. Holmquest -- physician/electrical engineer (M.D., Baylor; Ph.D., Baylor)
6. William B. Lenoir -- electrical engineer (Ph.D., MIT)
7. J. Anthony Llewellyn -- chemist (Ph.D., University College Cardiff) -- Welsh-born, naturalized; resigned Sept 1968
8. F. Story Musgrave -- physician/physiologist (M.D., Columbia; M.S., Kentucky) -- former USMC enlisted
9. Brian T. O'Leary -- astronomer (Ph.D., UC Berkeley) -- resigned April 1968
10. Robert A. R. Parker -- astronomer (Ph.D., Caltech)
11. William E. Thornton -- physician/physicist (M.D., UNC; B.S. physics, UNC) -- exceeded age limit, received waiver

## KEY DIVERGENCES FROM MERCURY SEVEN SELECTION

For context within the Apollo Aging Anomaly project:

- **Zero test pilots.** Every Mercury astronaut was an active military test pilot. Group 6 had zero.
- **All doctorates.** Mercury required 1,500 hours flight time and test pilot school. Group 6 required a doctoral degree.
- **Majority fully civilian.** 10 of 11 had no military background at all. Musgrave had enlisted USMC service but was selected as a scientist.
- **No flight experience required.** Mercury candidates needed extensive jet time. Group 6 candidates received a T-38 back-seat ride during selection as an assessment, not a prerequisite.
- **NAS-NRC screened for scientific merit.** Mercury candidates were screened by military/NASA for operational capability.
- **Age limit relaxed.** Mercury had strict age limit of 40 (effectively younger due to experience requirements). Group 6 abolished/relaxed the age limit, allowing waivers for Henize (born 1926) and Thornton (born 1929).
- **Physical standards lowered.** O'Leary specifically noted "the relaxation of physical requirements" as a reason for optimism about joining.
- **Two naturalized citizens.** Mercury required natural-born citizenship; Group 6 allowed naturalized citizens (Chapman, Llewellyn).
