## Key Findings

### What the USAF MOL Selection Process Consisted Of

**Two distinct pathways:**

1. **First group (1964-1965):** No applications accepted. Yeager and Lt. Col. "Buck" Buchanan secretly screened all 85 ARPS graduates, went to Washington with records, convened a selection board. 15 candidates sent to Brooks AFB for week-long medical evaluation. 9 initially identified, reduced to 8 after one had "some sort of a medical problem" (per Truly). Announced November 12, 1965.

2. **Second and third groups (1965-1967):** Formal application process adopted after complaints. 500+ applications received for second group; 100 forwarded to USAF HQ; 25 sent to Brooks AFB for physical evaluation (Jan-Feb 1966); 5 selected. Third group drawn from finalists of second group who had not yet completed ARPS; 4 selected after completing the course.

### Medical Evaluation at Brooks AFB

- Conducted at USAF School of Aerospace Medicine, Brooks Air Force Base, San Antonio, Texas
- Duration: one week
- Described as "similar to those conducted for NASA astronaut groups"
- The Truly oral history confirms Brooks AFB physical was "a long physical that was required by the test pilot school" (not specific to MOL; it was a prerequisite for ARPS admission itself)
- **No source provides a specific itemized list of Brooks AFB medical tests for MOL candidates** — this is a significant gap

### Physical Requirements

- Height: not taller than 6 feet (Gemini-B capsule constraint)
- Age: born not earlier than December 1, 1931 (first group; approximately age 33)
- No civilians permitted

### Educational Requirements

- Graduate of a service academy OR bachelor's degree in engineering, natural science, physical science, or biological science
- All MOL astronauts were ARPS graduates (mandatory)

### NASA Transfer Process

- **No separate NASA selection process was conducted**
- Age cutoff: 35 or younger at time of cancellation (June 1969)
- Decision made between Mueller (wanted to maintain USAF relations) and Slayton/Gilruth (didn't want more astronauts)
- Truly explicitly states: "I never filled out an application [to NASA]. So I'm the only person who has ever flown in space that never applied."
- **No evidence of additional NASA medical screening at transfer** — all sources describe the transfer as administrative

### Comparison to NASA Selection

The MOL medical evaluation at Brooks AFB is described as "similar to" NASA's astronaut group medicals, which were also conducted at Brooks and at Lovelace Clinic. However, the ARPS requirement meant all MOL candidates had already passed a military flight physical at Brooks AFB as a prerequisite for test pilot school admission. This represents a double screening: once for ARPS entry, and again specifically for MOL selection.

## Methodological Notes

- The MOL program was classified until NRO declassification in October 2015. Sources before that date have limited information on the actual selection process.
- The medical evaluation details for MOL candidates are significantly less documented than for NASA astronaut groups. The Mercury selection fact sheet (NASA Release 59-113) provides exhaustive detail on Lovelace and WADC testing; no equivalent document has been found for the Brooks AFB MOL evaluations.
- The phrase "similar to those conducted for NASA astronaut groups" (from Wikipedia, sourced to NRO/USAF records) is the closest characterization available of the Brooks AFB medical battery.
- The ARPS prerequisite effectively added a pre-screening layer: all MOL candidates had already been through a rigorous military flight physical and graduated from an elite test pilot school before being considered for MOL.
