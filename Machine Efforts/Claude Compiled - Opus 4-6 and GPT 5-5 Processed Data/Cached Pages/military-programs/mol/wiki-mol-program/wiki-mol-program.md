# Wikipedia: Manned Orbiting Laboratory
## Cached 2026-05-19

The MOL was a classified U.S. Air Force human spaceflight program developed during the 1960s as a successor to the canceled X-20 Dyna-Soar. Though publicly presented as a space laboratory for military experiments, its actual primary mission was reconnaissance — a black project codenamed "Dorian."

**Timeline & Status**: Announced December 10, 1963; canceled June 1969 without any crewed missions

**Personnel**: Seventeen astronauts selected across three groups, including Major Robert H. Lawrence Jr., "the first African-American to be chosen as an astronaut." Seven later transferred to NASA and flew on the Space Shuttle.

**Hardware**: The Gemini B spacecraft (derived from NASA's Gemini) would launch atop a Titan IIIM rocket. The laboratory module measured 21.92 meters long with 11.3 cubic meters of pressurized volume for two-person crews on 30-day missions.

## Selection Criteria
USAF established selection criteria on 15 September 1965. Candidates had to be "qualified military pilots; Graduates of the ARPS; Serving officers, recommended by their commanding officers; and Holding U.S. citizenship from birth."

## Group 1 (Announced November 12, 1965)
Eight pilots selected:
- Major Michael J. Adams (USAF)
- Major Albert H. Crews Jr. (USAF)
- Lieutenant John L. Finley (USN)
- Captain Richard E. Lawyer (USAF)
- Captain Lachlan Macleay (USAF)
- Captain Francis G. Neubeck (USAF)
- Major James M. Taylor (USAF)
- Lieutenant Richard H. Truly (USN)

## Group 2 (Announced June 17, 1966)
Five pilots selected from over 500 applications:
- Captain Karol J. Bobko (USAF)
- Lieutenant Robert L. Crippen (USN)
- Captain C. Gordon Fullerton (USAF)
- Captain Henry W. Hartsfield Jr. (USAF)
- Captain Robert F. Overmyer (USMC)

## Group 3 (Announced June 30, 1967)
Four pilots selected:
- Major James A. Abrahamson (USAF)
- Lieutenant Colonel Robert T. Herres (USAF)
- Major Robert H. Lawrence Jr. (USAF)
- Major Donald H. Peterson (USAF)

## Post-Cancellation
Seven astronauts transferred to NASA in August 1969 as NASA Astronaut Group 7, all of whom eventually flew in space on the Space Shuttle between 1981 and 1985.

Three had already departed before cancellation:
- John L. Finley — returned to the Navy (April 1968)
- Michael J. Adams — transferred to the X-15 (killed November 15, 1967)
- Robert H. Lawrence — died during training (December 8, 1967)

Seven exceeded NASA's age limit of 35 and were not transferred:
- Robert T. Herres
- James A. Abrahamson
- Lachlan Macleay
- Richard E. Lawyer
- James M. Taylor
- Albert H. Crews (NASA hired him separately as a test pilot)
- Francis G. Neubeck

## Cancellation Rationale
Newly inaugurated President Nixon sought defense spending cuts. The competing CIA Hexagon satellite program and rapidly improving automated reconnaissance systems (KH-8 Gambit 3) made MOL's cost-benefit increasingly questionable.
