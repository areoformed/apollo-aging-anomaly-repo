# Milton O. Thompson — Wikipedia Cache

**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Orville_Thompson
**Cached:** 2026-05-19

**Full Name:** Milton Orville Thompson
**Born:** May 4, 1926, Crookston, Minnesota
**Died:** August 6, 1993, Lancaster, California (age 67; natural causes)

**Education:**
- BS Engineering, University of Washington, 1953

**Military Service:**
- US Navy pilot trainee beginning age 19
- Served in China and Japan during WWII
- Six years active Naval service
- Remained in Naval Reserve during college
- Achieved rank of Lieutenant Commander, US Naval Reserve

**Career:**
- Flight test engineer at Boeing Aircraft Company, Seattle (2 years post-graduation)
- Hired at NACA/NASA flight research facility, March 19, 1956
- Became research pilot, January 1958
- X-20 Dyna-Soar program: 1960-1963
- First person to fly a lifting body, August 16, 1963 (M2-F1)
- X-15 program: 14 flights (1963-1965), max speed 3,712 mph (Mach 5.48), peak altitude 214,100 feet
  - Peak altitude below 50-mile astronaut threshold (264,000 ft) — did NOT qualify for astronaut wings
- Chief of Research Projects, 1969
- Chief Engineer and Director of Research Projects, NASA Dryden, 1975-1993

**Family:** Married Therese Beytebiere; one daughter (Brett) and four sons
