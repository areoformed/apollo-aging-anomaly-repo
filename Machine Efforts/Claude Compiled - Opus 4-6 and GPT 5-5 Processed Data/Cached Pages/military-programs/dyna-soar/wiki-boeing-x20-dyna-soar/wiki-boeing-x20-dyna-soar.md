## Program Basics
The Boeing X-20 Dyna-Soar was a "Dynamic Soarer" spaceplane developed by the United States Air Force from October 24, 1957, to December 10, 1963. The program cost $660 million (approximately $6.94 billion in 2024 dollars) and was cancelled just as spacecraft construction began.

## Design Concept
Unlike contemporary space capsules such as Mercury or Vostok, the Dyna-Soar was aircraft-like. It could reach orbital speeds equivalent to intercontinental ballistic missile velocity, glide to Earth under pilot control like an airplane, and land at conventional airbases.

## Historical Origins
The underlying concept originated in Nazi Germany during World War II through Eugen Sanger and Irene Bredt's 1941 Silbervogel proposal. Post-war, German scientists including Walter Dornberger and Krafft Ehricke brought Silbervogel knowledge to Bell Aircraft via Operation Paperclip. In 1952, they proposed the "Bomber Missile" or "BoMi."

## Development Timeline
- October 1957: USAF consolidated Hywards, Brass Bell, and RoBo into unified Dyna-Soar project (Weapons System 464L)
- March 1958: Nine aerospace companies competed
- June 1959: Boeing won the contract
- Late 1961: Titan III selected as launch vehicle
- December 10, 1963: Program cancelled

## Pilot Selection
In April 1960, seven astronauts were secretly assigned:
- Neil Armstrong (NASA, 1960-1962)
- William H. "Bill" Dana (NASA, 1960-1962)
- Henry C. Gordon (Air Force, 1960-1963)
- Pete Knight (Air Force, 1960-1963)
- Russell L. Rogers (Air Force, 1960-1963)
- Milt Thompson (NASA, 1960-1963)
- James W. Wood (Air Force, 1960-1963)

Armstrong and Dana departed mid-1962. Albert Crews joined September 1962.

## Cancellation
Cancelled December 10, 1963. Same day, Air Force announced Manned Orbiting Laboratory (itself later cancelled). Research influenced the Space Shuttle program.
