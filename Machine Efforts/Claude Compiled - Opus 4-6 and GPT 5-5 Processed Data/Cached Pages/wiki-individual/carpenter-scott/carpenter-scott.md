# Scott Carpenter - Biographical Data

**Full Name:** Malcolm Scott Carpenter

**Birth:** May 1, 1925, Boulder, Colorado

**Death:** October 10, 2013, Denver, Colorado (aged 88)

**Cause of Death:** Stroke (suffered September 2013; hospitalized at Swedish Medical Center, then Denver Hospice)

---

## Military Service

**Branch:** United States Navy

**Rank:** Commander

**Service Timeline:**
- Commissioned 1949
- Naval aviator, flying Lockheed P-2 Neptune with Patrol Squadron 6 (VP-6) during Korean War and Cold War
- Test pilot training at U.S. Naval Test Pilot School, NAS Patuxent River (1954)
- Air Intelligence Officer, USS Hornet (1958)
- Retired from Navy: 1969

---

## Education

**Degree:** Bachelor of Science in Aeronautical Engineering, University of Colorado, Boulder
- *Note:* Carpenter missed final heat transfer exam due to washed-out bridge; degree awarded May 29, 1962, after spaceflight, as "his subsequent training as an astronaut more than made up for the deficiency"

---

## Pre-NASA Career

- Naval aviator flying reconnaissance and anti-submarine warfare missions along Soviet and Chinese coasts
- Test pilot at NAS Patuxent River (1954-1957), flying AD Skyraider, Martin P4M Mercator, F9F Panther, F11F Tiger, A3D Skywarrior
- Attended Navy General Line School, Monterey (1957)
- Naval Air Intelligence School, NAS Anacostia, Washington D.C.

---

## NASA Career

**Selection:** Mercury Seven astronauts, April 1959

**Role:** Backup pilot to John Glenn on Mercury-Atlas 6

**Mission:** Mercury-Atlas 7 (Aurora 7)
- **Date:** May 24, 1962
- **Duration:** 4 hours, 56 minutes
- **Achievements:** Second American to orbit Earth; first American to eat solid food in space; identified "fireflies" as frozen particles, renaming them "frostflies"
- **Notable Issue:** Malfunctioning pitch horizon scanner caused 250-mile overshoot of planned Atlantic splashdown; Carpenter manually compensated, demonstrating backup systems effectiveness

---

## Time in Space

**Total:** 4 hours, 56 minutes (single orbital mission)

---

## Post-NASA Career

**SEALAB Project (1964-1969):**
- Leave of absence from NASA to join U.S. Navy SEALAB program as aquanaut
- July 1964: Motorcycle accident in Bermuda during training—compound fracture in right arm, left knee damage
- August-September 1965: 28 days living on ocean floor in SEALAB II at 205 feet depth off California coast
- Injury from scorpion fish spine during SEALAB II
- Executive Assistant to Director, Manned Spacecraft Center
- Director of Aquanaut Operations for SEALAB III (1967)
- Retired from NASA: August 3, 1967

**Later Career:**
- Consultant to sports/diving manufacturers and film industry on space flight and oceanography
- Founded Sea Sciences, Inc. (developed ocean resource programs)
- Television commercials for Oldsmobile, Standard Oil of California, Nintendo, Atari
- Author: *The Steel Albatross* (1991); *Deep Flight* (1994); *For Spacious Skies* autobiography (2003, co-written with daughter Kristen Stoever)

---

## Evidence of Vitality at Age 80+

**2008 (age 83):** Participated in Veterans Day ceremony aboard USS Intrepid with President George W. Bush and Buzz Aldrin

**2012 (age 87):** Joined John Glenn and former NASA staff to celebrate 50th anniversary of Glenn's mission; when asked about legacy, replied: "I was an astronaut and an aquanaut"

**2013 (age 88):** Suffered stroke in September; maintained married status (to fourth wife Patricia Barrett since 1999); survived by four sons, two daughters
