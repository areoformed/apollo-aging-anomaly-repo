# Thomas P. Stafford: Biographical Data

**Full Name:** Thomas Patten Stafford

**Birth:** September 17, 1930, Weatherford, Oklahoma

**Death:** March 18, 2024, Satellite Beach, Florida (aged 93)

**Cause of Death:** Liver cancer

---

## Military Service
- **Branch:** United States Air Force
- **Final Rank:** Lieutenant General
- Commissioned as second lieutenant (1952); flew F-86 Sabre interceptor missions in Arctic and West Germany
- Air Force Test Pilot School (1958), finishing first in class

---

## Education
- **United States Naval Academy:** Class of 1952, BS in Engineering with honors
- **Air Force Test Pilot School** (Edwards AFB, 1958)
- Honorary doctorates: Oklahoma City University, Western State University, Emerson College, Embry-Riddle

---

## Pre-NASA Career
- Test pilot and flight instructor at Edwards AFB
- Created the first civilian instructor position
- Co-authored technical handbooks on flight testing

---

## NASA Career

**Selection:** NASA Astronaut Group 2 (1962)
**Chief of Astronaut Office:** 1969-1971

**Missions:**
1. **Gemini VI-A** (December 15-16, 1965) — Pilot; rendezvoused with Gemini VII
2. **Gemini IX-A** (June 3-6, 1966) — Command pilot; orbital rendezvous maneuvers
3. **Apollo 10** (May 18-26, 1969) — Commander; descended lunar module to 9 miles above Moon; crew set speed record of 24,791 mph
4. **Apollo-Soyuz Test Project** (July 15-24, 1975) — Commander; first U.S.-Soviet joint space mission; first general officer to fly in space

**Total Time in Space:** 21 days, 3 hours, 42 minutes

---

## Post-NASA Career
- Commander, Air Force Flight Test Center (1975-1979)
- Deputy Chief of Staff; involved in F-117 and B-2 programs
- Retired USAF: November 1, 1979 (Lieutenant General)
- Board positions: Omega SA, Gulfstream Aerospace
- Founded consulting firm Stafford, Burke, and Hecker
- Led NASA's Synthesis Committee (1990) on lunar/Mars missions
- Technical advisor for Space Station Freedom and Shuttle-Mir program (1992+)
- Autobiography *We Have Capture* (2002)

---

## Evidence of Vitality at Age 80+
- Gave eulogy in Russian at cosmonaut Alexei Leonov's funeral (October 2019, age 88)
- Received General James E. Hill Lifetime Space Achievement Award (2019, age 88)
- Maintained active hobbies: hunting, weight lifting, gliding, scuba diving, fishing, swimming
- No documented physical decline before cancer death at 93
- Lived to age 93
