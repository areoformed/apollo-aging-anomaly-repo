# Biographical Data: Gus Grissom

**Full Name:** Virgil Ivan "Gus" Grissom

**Date of Birth:** April 3, 1926

**Date of Death:** January 27, 1967 (aged 40)

**Cause of Death:** Asphyxiation in spacecraft fire during Apollo 1 pre-launch test at Cape Kennedy, Florida

---

## Military Service
- **Branch:** United States Air Force
- **Final Rank:** Lieutenant Colonel
- **Service Summary:**
  - Enlisted in U.S. Army Air Forces (1944)
  - Commissioned as Second Lieutenant (March 1951)
  - Promoted to First Lieutenant (March 1952)
  - Promoted to Captain (May 1957)
  - Korean War combat pilot: 100 missions in F-86 Sabre
  - Test pilot at Edwards Air Force Base and Wright-Patterson AFB

---

## Education
- **Bachelor of Science in Mechanical Engineering** — Purdue University (February 1950)
- **Bachelor of Science in Aeromechanics** — Air Force Institute of Technology, Wright-Patterson AFB (1956)
- **U.S. Air Force Test Pilot School** — Edwards Air Force Base (completed May 1957)

---

## Pre-NASA Career
- Bus Manufacturing Worker — Carpenter Body Works, Mitchell, Indiana (1945)
- Flight Instructor — Bryan AFB, Texas (post-Korea)
- Test Pilot — Wright-Patterson AFB, fighter branch (1957 onward)

---

## NASA Career

**Selection:** NASA Group 1 (Mercury Seven) — April 13, 1959

**Missions:**
1. **Mercury-Redstone 4** (July 21, 1961)
   - Sub-orbital flight: 15 minutes 37 seconds
   - Second American in space
   - Spacecraft: *Liberty Bell 7*
   - Incident: Hatch unexpectedly opened post-splashdown; spacecraft sank

2. **Gemini 3** (March 23, 1965)
   - Command pilot; crewed with John W. Young
   - Duration: 4 hours, 52 minutes, 31 seconds
   - First person to fly in space twice
   - Spacecraft nicknamed *Molly Brown*

3. **Apollo 1** (planned February 21, 1967)
   - Commander, with Ed White and Roger B. Chaffee
   - Mission cancelled due to fatal fire during January 27, 1967 pre-launch test

**Total Time in Space:** 5 hours, 7 minutes

**Flight Hours:** 4,600 total; 3,500 in jet aircraft

---

## Awards & Honors
- Distinguished Flying Cross (Korean War service)
- Air Medal with oak leaf cluster
- NASA Distinguished Service Medal (twice)
- Congressional Space Medal of Honor (posthumous, 1978)
- Honorary doctorate from Florida Institute of Technology (1962)
- International Space Hall of Fame (1981)
- National Aviation Hall of Fame (1987)
- U.S. Astronaut Hall of Fame (1990, posthumous)

---

## Post-NASA Career
None; Grissom died during his third mission preparation.
