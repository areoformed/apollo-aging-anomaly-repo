# Henry Warren Hartsfield Jr. - Biographical Data

**Full Name:** Henry Warren Hartsfield Jr.

**Date of Birth:** November 21, 1933, Birmingham, Alabama

**Date of Death:** July 17, 2014 (aged 80), Houston, Texas

**Cause of Death:** Complications from back surgery

---

## Military Service
- **Branch:** United States Air Force
- **Rank:** Colonel
- **Service Period:** 1955-1977 (active duty, 22+ years)
- 53rd Tactical Fighter Squadron, Bitburg, West Germany
- U.S. Air Force Test Pilot School graduate (Edwards AFB)
- Test Pilot School instructor
- USAF Manned Orbiting Laboratory (MOL) astronaut (1966-1969)
- Flight experience: Over 7,400 hours across multiple jet aircraft

---

## Education
- **B.S.** (1954): Physics, Auburn University
- Graduate work at Duke University (physics) and Air Force Institute of Technology (astronautics)
- **M.S.** (1971): Engineering Science, University of Tennessee, Knoxville
- **Honorary Doctor of Science** (1986): Auburn University

---

## Pre-NASA Career
Test pilot and USAF officer; MOL program astronaut candidate until program cancellation in June 1969.

---

## NASA Career Highlights
- Selected for NASA Astronaut Group 7 (September 1969) — transferred from cancelled MOL program
- Support crew for Apollo 16; Skylab 2, 3, 4
- STS-1 CAPCOM
- Backup pilot for STS-2 and STS-3

---

## Missions Flown
1. **STS-4** (June 27-July 4, 1982): Pilot; Space Shuttle Columbia's fourth orbital test flight
2. **STS-41-D** (August 30-September 5, 1984): Spacecraft Commander; Space Shuttle Discovery maiden flight
3. **STS-61-A** (October 30-November 6, 1985): Spacecraft Commander; Space Shuttle Challenger; first 8-person crew mission

---

## Time in Space
483 hours (20 days, 2 hours, 50 minutes)

---

## Post-NASA Career
- Deputy Chief, Astronaut Office (1986-1987)
- Deputy Director, Flight Crew Operations, Johnson Space Center (1987-1989)
- Director, Technical Integration and Analysis Division, NASA HQ (1989-1990)
- Multiple Space Station management roles (1990-1998)
- NASA Retirement: March 1998
- Vice President, Aerospace Engineering Services, Raytheon Company (1998-2005)

---

## Evidence of Vitality at Age 80+
No direct evidence of speeches, interviews, flying, or active professional engagements near age 80 documented in Wikipedia. Retired from Raytheon in 2005 (age 71). Inducted into U.S. Astronaut Hall of Fame in 2006 (age 72). Died at age 80 from complications of back surgery.
