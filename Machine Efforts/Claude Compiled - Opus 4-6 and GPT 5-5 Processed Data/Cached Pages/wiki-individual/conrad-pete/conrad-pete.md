# Pete Conrad - Biographical Data

**Full Name:** Charles "Pete" Conrad Jr.

**Birth:** June 2, 1930, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania

**Death:** July 8, 1999 (aged 69), Ojai, California

**Cause of Death:** Internal injuries from a motorcycle accident while traveling from Huntington Beach to Monterey, California

---

## Military Service
- **Branch:** United States Navy
- **Rank:** Captain (achieved December 11, 1969)
- Commissioned as Ensign (1953) via Naval ROTC
- Naval Aviator designation: September 1954
- Fighter pilot and test pilot roles

---

## Education
- **Bachelor of Science in Aeronautical Engineering:** Princeton University (1953)
- Senior thesis: "The Design of a Turbo-Jet Military Advanced Trainer"
- Completed U.S. Naval Test Pilot School, Class 20 (1958)

---

## Pre-NASA Career
- Naval fighter pilot on aircraft carriers
- Flight instructor in Navy flight schools
- Test pilot assignments

---

## NASA Career
- **Selection:** NASA Astronaut Group 2 (June 1962)
- **Total spaceflight time:** 49 days, 3 hours, 38 minutes
- **EVAs:** Four, totaling 12 hours 46 minutes (7h 45m lunar surface, 5h 1m Earth orbit)

**Missions:**
1. **Gemini 5** (1965) — Pilot; set eight-day space endurance record
2. **Gemini 11** (1966) — Commander; achieved highest-apogee crewed Earth orbit (1,369 km)
3. **Apollo 12** (November 1969) — Commander; third person to walk on the Moon
4. **Skylab 2** (1973) — Commander; first crewed Skylab mission; repaired station damage

---

## Post-NASA Career (Retired 1973)
- Vice President, American Television and Communications Company
- McDonnell Douglas (1976-1990s): multiple VP and senior VP roles
- Consultant for Delta Clipper experimental spacecraft
- February 1996: Participated in record-breaking around-the-world Learjet flight (49 hours, 26 minutes, 8 seconds)
- June 1999: Appeared on ABC News Nightline discussing Space Shuttle value
- PBS NOVA interview advocating Mars and asteroid missions
