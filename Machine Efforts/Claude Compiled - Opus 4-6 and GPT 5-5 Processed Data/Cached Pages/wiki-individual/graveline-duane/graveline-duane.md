**Full Name:** Duane Edgar "Doc" Graveline

**Date of Birth:** March 2, 1931 (Newport, Vermont)

**Date of Death:** September 5, 2016 (age 85, Merritt Island, Florida)

**Cause of Death:** Not specified in article

---

## Military Service
- **Branch:** United States Air Force (USAF); later United States Army
- **Rank:** Major (USAF), Colonel (USA)

---

## Education
- BS from University of Vermont (1951)
- MD from University of Vermont College of Medicine (1955)
- MPH from Johns Hopkins University (1958)
- Flight Surgeon rating (February 1957)
- Aerospace Medical residency completed (July 1960, Brooks AFB)

---

## Pre-NASA Career
- USAF Medical Service officer
- Flight surgeon and aerospace medicine specialist
- Research scientist focused on weightlessness deconditioning and countermeasures
- NASA flight controller for Mercury and Gemini missions
- Authored ten professional publications on biological deconditioning

---

## NASA Career Highlights
- Selected in NASA's fourth astronaut group (June 1965)
- Resigned August 18, 1965 — "the first astronaut to resign prior to being assigned a mission"
- Reason: impending divorce, as noted in Deke Slayton's memoir
- Stayed with NASA three months post-resignation as a doctor in Houston

---

## Missions Flown
None

## Time in Space
Zero

---

## Post-NASA Career
- Family medicine practitioner in Burlington, Vermont (~20 years)
- Flight surgeon for Vermont Army National Guard
- Director of Medical Operations at Kennedy Space Center for first four Space Shuttle launches (1981 onward)
- Medical license suspended (1987) over unaccounted narcotic pills, restored (1989)
- License permanently revoked (1994) for alleged sexual abuse of five children
- Author of 15 self-published medical and science fiction thrillers (retirement career)
- Contributor to 2006 book _NASA's Scientist-Astronauts_
