# William Edgar Thornton - Biographical Data

**Full Name:** William Edgar Thornton

**Date of Birth:** April 14, 1929

**Date of Death:** January 11, 2021 (aged 91)

**Cause of Death:** Not specified in source material

---

## Military Service
- **Branch:** United States Air Force
- **Rank:** Officer (completed ROTC training)
- Served as officer-in-charge of Instrumentation Lab at Flight Test Air Proving Ground
- Later assigned to USAF Aerospace Medical Division at Brooks Air Force Base
- Completed Primary Flight Surgeon's training in 1964

---

## Education
- **Bachelor of Science** (1952): Physics, University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill
- **Doctor of Medicine** (1963): University of North Carolina
- Flight training completed at Reese Air Force Base, Texas

---

## Pre-NASA Career
- Chief engineer, electronics division, Del Mar Engineering Labs, Los Angeles (1956-1959)
- Organized and directed Avionics Division
- Medical internship at Wilford Hall USAF Hospital, Lackland AFB, Texas (1964)
- Space medicine research at Brooks Air Force Base

---

## NASA Career Highlights
- Selected as scientist-astronaut, NASA Astronaut Group 6 (August 1967)
- Physician crew member on Skylab Medical Experiments Altitude Test (56-day simulation)
- Principal investigator for Skylab experiments on mass measurement and hemodynamics
- First documented shift and loss of fluid changes in body posture size in spaceflight
- Developed first mass measuring devices for space
- Invented shuttle treadmill for in-flight exercise
- Holds more than 60 patents spanning military systems through medical devices
- Became oldest person to fly in space (at time of STS-8)

---

## Missions Flown
1. **STS-8** (August 30 - September 5, 1983): Challenger's third flight; first night launch/landing; studied nervous system adaptation to weightlessness; 98 orbits; 145 hours, 8 minutes, 4 seconds
2. **STS-51B/Spacelab-3** (April 29 - May 6, 1985): Responsible for first animal payload on shuttle mission; medical investigations; 110 orbits; 169 hours, 39 minutes

Over 313 hours total

## Post-NASA Career
- Retired from NASA May 31, 1994
- Clinical Assistant Professor, Department of Medicine, University of Texas Medical Branch, Galveston
- Adjunct Professor, University of Houston-Clear Lake
- 2010: Donated NASA papers and archives to North Carolina State Archives
