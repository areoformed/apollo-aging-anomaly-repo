# Robert Franklyn Overmyer - Biographical Data

**Full Name:** Robert Franklyn Overmyer

**Date of Birth:** July 14, 1936, Lorain, Ohio

**Date of Death:** March 22, 1996 (aged 59), near Duluth International Airport, Minnesota

**Cause of Death:** Aircraft crash while testing the Cirrus VK-30 during stall recovery testing at aft center of gravity limits

---

## Military Service
- **Branch:** United States Marine Corps
- **Rank:** Colonel, USMC (Retired)
- Active duty from January 1958
- Completed Navy flight training in Kingsville, Texas
- Assigned to VMA-214 in November 1959
- Served with Marine Maintenance Squadron 17 at MCAS Iwakuni, Japan
- Attended U.S. Air Force Test Pilot School at Edwards Air Force Base
- Logged over 7,500 flight hours with over 6,000 in jet aircraft

---

## Education
- **Bachelor of Science** (1958): Physics, Baldwin-Wallace University
- **Master of Science** (1964): Aeronautics (aeronautical engineering focus), Naval Postgraduate School
- **Honorary Doctor of Philosophy** (1982): Baldwin-Wallace College

---

## Pre-NASA Career
- U.S. Air Force Test Pilot School
- Selected as astronaut for the Manned Orbiting Laboratory (MOL) program in 1966

---

## NASA Career Highlights
- Selected for NASA Astronaut Group 7 (1969) — transferred from cancelled MOL program
- Engineering development on Skylab Program (1969-1971)
- Support crew, Apollo 17 (1971-1972); launch capsule communicator
- Support crew, Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (1973-1975); NASA CAPCOM in Moscow
- ALT program (1976); T-38 Talon chase pilot
- Deputy vehicle manager for Space Shuttle Columbia (1979-1980)
- Lead investigator into Challenger disaster (1986)

---

## Missions Flown
1. **STS-5** (November 11-16, 1982): Pilot; first fully operational shuttle flight; first four-person crew; first deployment of commercial communications satellites; 122 hours
2. **STS-51-B** (April 29 - May 6, 1985): Commander; Spacelab-3 mission; 110 orbits at 190 nautical miles altitude

---

## Time in Space
12 days, 2 hours, 22 minutes

---

## Post-NASA Career
- Consulting business: Mach Twenty Five International, Inc.
- Consultant for major aerospace corporations and NBC
- Columnist for British publication Space Flight News
- McDonnell Douglas Aerospace (March 1988 - April 1995): Led crew and operations activities for Space Station Team for seven years
- Joined Cirrus Design in Duluth, Minnesota (November 1995)
- Conducted stall testing for Cirrus VK-30 kit aircraft
- Early certification testing for Cirrus SR20

---

## Evidence of Vitality at Age 80+
Not applicable — Overmyer died at age 59 in a test flight accident. He was professionally active as a test pilot at time of death.

## Personal Life
Spouse: Katherine. Three children. Buried at Arlington National Cemetery.
