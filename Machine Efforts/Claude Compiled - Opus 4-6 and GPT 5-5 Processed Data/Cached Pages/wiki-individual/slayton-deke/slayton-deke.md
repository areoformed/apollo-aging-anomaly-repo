# Deke Slayton Biographical Data

**Full Name:** Donald Kent "Deke" Slayton

**Birth:** March 1, 1924, near Leon, Wisconsin

**Death:** June 13, 1993, League City, Texas (aged 69)

**Cause of Death:** Malignant brain tumor (diagnosed 1992)

---

## Military Service
- **Branch:** United States Air Force
- **Rank:** Major
- **Service Period:** WWII through 1963 (resigned commission); recalled to active duty status for NASA missions
- **WWII Service:** 56 combat sorties in Europe (B-25 Mitchell), 7 combat missions in Pacific (A-26 Invader)

---

## Education
- **University of Minnesota:** Bachelor of Science in Aeronautical Engineering (1949)
- **U.S. Air Force Test Pilot School:** Class 55C (graduated December 1955)

---

## Pre-NASA Career
- Boeing Aircraft Corporation engineer (1949-1951): worked on B-52 Stratofortress and KC-97 Stratofreighter
- Minnesota Air National Guard pilot
- Test pilot at Edwards Air Force Base (1955-1959): evaluated F-101, F-102, F-104, F-105, F-106 aircraft

---

## NASA Career Highlights
- Selected as Mercury Seven astronaut (April 2, 1959)
- Scheduled for Mercury-Atlas 7 mission; grounded March 15, 1962, due to "idiopathic paroxysmal atrial fibrillation"
- Chief of Astronaut Office (1962-1963)
- Director of Flight Crew Operations (1966-1974)
- Medically cleared to fly March 13, 1972
- Apollo-Soyuz Test Project docking module pilot (1975)

**Mission:**
- **Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (July 1975)**
  - Time in space: 9 days, 1 hour, 28 minutes
  - Age at flight: 51 (oldest astronaut to fly in space at that time)
  - Crew: Thomas P. Stafford (commander), Vance D. Brand (command module pilot), Deke Slayton

---

## Post-NASA Career
- Retired from NASA: February 27, 1982 (7,164 flight hours logged)
- President of Space Services Inc. (Houston-based commercial rocket company)
- Mission director for Conestoga rocket (first privately funded spacecraft, September 9, 1982)
- President, International Formula One Pylon Air Racing
- Co-wrote autobiography *Deke!* with Michael Cassutt (published 1994, posthumously)
- Co-authored *Moon Shot* with Alan Shepard (1994)
