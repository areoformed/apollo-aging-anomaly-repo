# Gordon Cooper: Biographical Data

**Full Name:** Leroy Gordon Cooper Jr.

**Birth:** March 6, 1927, in Shawnee, Oklahoma

**Death:** October 4, 2004 (aged 77) in Ventura, California

**Cause of Death:** Heart failure

---

## Military Service
- **Branch:** United States Air Force (USAF)
- **Rank:** Colonel
- **Service Details:** Commissioned as second lieutenant in U.S. Army (June 1949), transferred to USAF (September 1949). Served as fighter pilot in West Germany (1950-1954), flew F-84 Thunderjets and F-86 Sabres. Became flight commander of 525th Fighter Bomber Squadron. Test pilot training completed 1956. Logged over 2,000 flight hours before NASA selection.

---

## Education
- **University of Hawaii at Manoa** (partial attendance)
- **University of Maryland, College Park** (European Extension)
- **Air Force Institute of Technology (AFIT), Air University** — Bachelor of Science in Aerospace Engineering (August 28, 1956)

---

## Pre-NASA Career
- Test pilot and project manager at Edwards Air Force Base Flight Test Engineering Division (1956-1959)
- Tested F-102A and F-106B aircraft
- Achieved expertise in high-performance aircraft operations

---

## NASA Career Highlights

**Selection:** Mercury Seven astronaut (NASA Group 1, 1959)

**Key Missions:**
1. **Mercury-Atlas 9 (May 15-16, 1963)** — Piloted "Faith 7"
   - 34 hours, 19 minutes, 49 seconds in space
   - 22 orbits around Earth
   - First American to spend entire day in space
   - First to sleep in space
   - Last American launched on solo orbital mission
   - Despite severe equipment failures (power loss, rising CO2, gyroscope failures), completed manual re-entry guidance
   - Splashdown 6.4 kilometers from recovery ship USS Kearsarge

2. **Gemini 5 (August 21-29, 1965)** — Command Pilot with Pete Conrad
   - 190 hours 56 minutes in space (just under 8 days)
   - 120 orbits
   - Distance traveled: 5,331,745 kilometers (3,312,993 miles)
   - Set new space endurance record
   - First person to make second orbital flight
   - Successfully demonstrated astronauts could survive duration needed for Earth-Moon-Earth journey

**Total Time in Space:** 9 days, 9 hours, 14 minutes (222 hours)

**Supporting Roles:**
- Capsule communicator (CAPCOM) for Alan Shepard's Mercury-Redstone 3 and Scott Carpenter's Mercury-Atlas 7
- Backup pilot for Wally Schirra's Mercury-Atlas 8
- Backup commander for Apollo 10 (May 1969)

**NASA Retirement:** July 31, 1970

---

## Post-NASA Career
- President, Performance Unlimited, Inc. (1962-1967) — racing and marine engines, fiberglass boats
- President, GCR (1963-1967) — championship car design and testing
- President, Teletest (1965-1970) — advanced telemetry systems
- Technical consultant, Republic Corp., General Motors, Ford, Chrysler (1968-1974)
- President, Gordon Cooper & Associates, Inc. — consulting firm for aerospace and land development
- Vice President of Research and Development, The Walt Disney Company for EPCOT (1973-1975)
- Chief Executive, Galaxy Group, Inc. (1989) — small airplane design and improvement
- Published autobiography "Leap of Faith" (2000) with journalist Bruce Henderson
- Board memberships and consulting roles for multiple corporations
