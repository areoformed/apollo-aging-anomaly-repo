# John Watts Young - Biographical Data

**Full Name:** John Watts Young

**Birth:** September 24, 1930, San Francisco, California

**Death:** January 5, 2018, Houston, Texas (age 87)

**Cause of Death:** Complications from pneumonia

---

## Military Service
- **Branch:** United States Navy
- **Rank:** Captain (O-6)
- **Service Period:** Commissioned June 6, 1952; retired September 1976
- Korean War deployment aboard USS Laws; naval aviator; test pilot

---

## Education
- **BS in Aeronautical Engineering:** Georgia Institute of Technology (1952, graduated second in class)
- U.S. Naval Test Pilot School (1959, graduated second in class)

---

## Pre-NASA Career
- Naval aviator and fighter pilot
- Test pilot; set world time-to-climb records in F-4 Phantom II (1962)
- Naval Air Test Center: tested fighter weapons systems

---

## NASA Career

**Selection:** NASA Astronaut Group 2 (September 1962)
**Chief of Astronaut Office:** 1974-1987
**Associate Director (Technical), JSC:** 1996-2004
**Retirement from NASA:** December 31, 2004 (42 years service)

**Missions (6 total):**
1. **Gemini 3** (March 23, 1965) — Pilot
2. **Gemini 10** (July 18-21, 1966) — Commander
3. **Apollo 10** (May 18-26, 1969) — Command Module Pilot
4. **Apollo 16** (April 16-27, 1972) — Commander; 9th person to walk on Moon
5. **STS-1** (April 12-14, 1981) — Commander; first Space Shuttle orbital flight
6. **STS-9** (November 28-December 8, 1983) — Commander

**Total Time in Space:** 34 days, 19 hours, 39 minutes
**EVAs:** 3 total, 20 hours 14 minutes
**Flight Hours:** 15,275+ total

---

## Post-NASA Career
- Public speaker advocating for asteroid impact avoidance, lunar colonization, climate engineering
- Co-authored autobiography *Forever Young* with James R. Hansen (2012)

---

## Evidence of Vitality at Age 80+
- STS-1 25th anniversary appearance (April 2006, age 75 — just under 80)
- Met with STS-135 crew (November 2011, age 81)
- Published autobiography (2012, age 82)
- Maintained active engagement in space advocacy through mid-80s
- Died at 87 from pneumonia complications
