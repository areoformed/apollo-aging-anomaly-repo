# Neil Armstrong: Biographical Data

**Full Name:** Neil Alden Armstrong

**Date of Birth:** August 5, 1930

**Date of Death:** August 25, 2012 (aged 82)

**Cause of Death:** Complications from coronary bypass surgery

---

## Military Service

**Branch:** United States Navy
**Rank:** Lieutenant (Junior Grade), Naval Reserve

**Service Details:**
- Became midshipman February 24, 1949
- Qualified as naval aviator August 16, 1950
- Flew 78 missions over Korea (121 total air hours) in F9F Panther jets
- Received Air Medal with two gold stars, Korean Service Medal, National Defense Service Medal, UN Korea Medal
- Released from active duty August 23, 1952; remained in reserves until October 21, 1960

---

## Education

**Bachelor of Science:** Aeronautical Engineering, Purdue University (January 1955)

**Master of Science:** Aerospace Engineering, University of Southern California (1970)

---

## Pre-NASA Career

**Test Pilot (1955-1962):**
- NACA High-Speed Flight Station, Edwards Air Force Base
- Flew 200+ different aircraft models
- X-15 pilot (7 flights, reaching Mach 5.74 and 207,000+ feet altitude)
- Century Series fighter project pilot
- Flew Bell X-1B, Douglas D-558-2, and other experimental aircraft
- Accumulated 2,400 flying hours before joining NASA

---

## NASA Career

**Selection:** NASA Astronaut Group 2 (September 13, 1962) — "first civilian astronaut"

**Missions:**

1. **Gemini 8** (March 16, 1966)
   - Command pilot with David Scott
   - First spacecraft docking achieved
   - Mission aborted early due to thruster malfunction
   - Received NASA Exceptional Service Medal

2. **Apollo 11** (July 16-24, 1969)
   - Commander
   - First person to walk on the Moon (July 20-21, 1969)
   - "That's one small step for [a] man, one giant leap for mankind"

**Time in Space:** 8 days, 14 hours, 12 minutes

**EVA:** 1 moonwalk; 2 hours 31 minutes

---

## Post-NASA Career

**Academic:**
- Associate Professor, Department of Aerospace Engineering, University of Cincinnati (1971-1979)

**Government Service:**
- Apollo 13 accident investigation participant
- Rogers Commission investigator (Challenger disaster, 1986)

**Awards:**
- Presidential Medal of Freedom
- Congressional Space Medal of Honor (1978)
- Congressional Gold Medal (2009, with crewmates)
- National Aviation Hall of Fame (1979)

**Resignation from NASA:** August 1971
