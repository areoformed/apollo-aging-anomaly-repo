## What the Olympian literature is FOR here (qualitative scope only — verify before use)
The published finding (Sarna 1993 Finnish elite athletes; Antero-Jacquemin 2015 French
Olympians; Clarke 2012 Tour de France; Garatachea 2014) is, broadly, that elite
endurance/aerobic athletes show a MORTALITY ADVANTAGE vs the general population of roughly
the same order of magnitude as elite occupational health-selection — i.e., all-cause
SMR/HR meaningfully below 1, concentrated in cardiovascular and some cancer mortality.

**Bearing on THIS analysis:**
1. It bounds the LONGEVITY leg only. It says elite physical fitness can plausibly explain a
   survival advantage of the size seen here (SMR ~0.4–0.6) — which is exactly why the
   longevity leg of the Apollo claim is NOT the anomaly (and my analysis already finds the
   longevity leg null vs same-era flyers). So the Olympian control, if added, would
   REINFORCE "the survival advantage is ordinary for elite-selected people."
2. It does NOT touch the COGNITIVE-VITALITY leg, which is where the corrected-estimand
   anomaly actually lives. The Olympian cohorts have thin/no late-life cognition readouts;
   they cannot bound "do they stay cognitively sharp at 80+." So the Olympian comparator is
   structurally UNABLE to explain away the SuperAger-rate finding. State this explicitly.

## Recommendation to the author
- If you want the fitness control in the piece, source the Olympian SMR/HR numbers directly
